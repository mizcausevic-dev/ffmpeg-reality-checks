### Description

A phone records variable frame rate. Some tools want constant. What does FFmpeg do when you convert? Does it add pictures, and does the film change length? We build a synthetic variable frame rate clip and count.

The clip: 5 seconds of a 30 fps test pattern with frames removed unevenly, leaving 93 frames. ffprobe reports r_frame_rate 30/1 but avg_frame_rate 2790/151, about 18.5 fps. The gaps between frames are 33 ms, 67 ms and one of 933 ms.

Converting to MP4 with no frame rate option: on ffmpeg 6.1.1 the result is constant, 150 frames, 57 of them duplicates. On ffmpeg 8.1.2 the same command keeps all 93 frames and the uneven gaps. The default differs between these two builds; builds in between were not tested.

Say what you want: -fps_mode passthrough keeps 93 frames on both builds. -fps_mode cfr -r 30 gives 150 frames (6.1.1) or 151 (8.1.2); 25 fps gives 125 or 126; 60 fps gives 300 or 301.

The extra frames are copies. Converted losslessly, every variant holds 93 distinct pictures. Two counters agree: FFmpeg's dup= count and frames minus distinct picture hashes. The length stays about 5 seconds. A higher constant rate means more copies: on 6.1.1, 207 of 300 frames at 60 fps.
