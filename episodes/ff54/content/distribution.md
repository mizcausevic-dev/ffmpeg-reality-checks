### Description

You cut from second 2 and add a one second fade in. With -ss before -i the fade is there; with -ss after -i it vanishes. Where does the fade land? We cut 3 seconds out of a 6 second synthetic clip with -ss on each side of -i, add fade=t=in:st=0:d=1 and fade=t=out, and measure the brightness of every output frame.

Clip (lavfi, lossless x264, mkv, 96x72, 25 fps): 150 frames, every frame a flat card of luma 200. Black is luma 16. The cut is source seconds 2 to 5, 75 frames; the fade is one second, 25 frames. Eight outputs: a control with no fade, and fade in and fade out with -ss before -i and after -i.

Readers: ffmpeg framemd5 read with -copyts (one hash and one timestamp per decoded frame) and ffmpeg signalstats (YAVG per frame, printed by the metadata filter), raw decoded luma read in Node (mean of the whole luma plane, plus a check that each frame is flat), and ffprobe packet timestamps. They agree on all 8 outputs: 75 frames each, first frame at 0.000 s, a 0.04 s step.

Control: -ss 2 -t 3 with no fade gives 75 frames, all luma 200, one frame hash. Known positive: -ss 2 before -i (with -t 3) and fade=t=in:st=0:d=1 fade 25 frames: the first frame is luma 16 (black), the luma rises frame by frame, and frame 25 (1.00 s) and every later frame is back at 200.

The same filter with -ss after -i fades 0 frames: 75 frames at luma 200 with the control's hash. The fade ran over source seconds 0 to 1, which were thrown away. After -i the filter sees source time, so fade=t=in:st=2:d=1 after -i fades 25 frames, with exactly the same 75 frame hashes as st=0 before -i. The other way round it lands late: fade=t=in:st=2:d=1 before -i is black for 51 frames, then ramps for 24 frames and the clip ends at luma 193, never back to 200.

Fade out follows the same rule. fade=t=out:st=4:d=1 after -i has exactly the same 75 frame hashes as fade=t=out:st=2:d=1 before -i: full for 51 frames, then a 24 frame ramp down to luma 23 at the end. But fade=t=out:st=2:d=1 after -i fades within the first second and the clip stays black for 50 of its 75 frames, 2 of its 3 seconds.

The 13 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1. They also pass on 8.1.2, but there the build-dependent claims (exact counts, bytes, error text) are not asserted, so the film's numbers are 6.1.1 numbers.
