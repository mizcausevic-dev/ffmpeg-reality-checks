### Description

Why do thin lines look jagged or vanish when I scale down? Which scale flag is to blame: neighbor, bilinear, bicubic, lanczos or area? We draw lines one pixel wide, scale them down by 3, and measure what is left.

Source (luma planes made in Node, fed to ffmpeg as raw yuv420p): a 312x24 card, background luma 60, twelve 1 px vertical lines of luma 200, one every 25 columns, so each line sits at one of the three phases of a 3x downscale (four lines per phase). Output 104x8.

Two readers: the raw output luma bytes read in Node, and ffmpeg's own signalstats (YMIN, YMAX, YAVG on a cropped window) with psnr against a reference computed in Node. They agree. Control: flags=area at an integer factor on a pseudo-random card equals the block averages computed in Node (2x identical, 3x largest difference 1 level, psnr 62.67 dB); the other flags are 29 to 140 levels away on the same card.

scale=...:flags=neighbor keeps a pixel or drops it: 4 of 12 lines survive at the full 200 (one phase in three) and 8 of 12 are gone, peak 60, the background. Shift a line by one pixel and it appears or disappears. Stripes of luma 0 and 255 scaled 2x come out all 0, and shifted by one pixel all 255; flags=area gives 128 both ways.

Every other flag keeps all 12 lines, but faint. area gives peak 107 at every phase (a third of the contrast, the exact block average). bilinear gives 91, 107, 91 over the three phases, bicubic 97, 107, 97, lanczos 99, 107, 99; at the weak phases the line is 2 pixels wide (bilinear 91 and 76). 22 to 34 percent of the contrast is left.

Ringing: next to a line lanczos goes down to 53 and bicubic to 56 (background 60); bilinear, area and neighbor stay at 60. On a hard edge from 60 to 200, bicubic and lanczos overshoot by 2 to 3 levels. Line luma sum, input = 1: area, bilinear and bicubic 1.01, lanczos 1.03 to 1.05, spline up to 1.06, neighbor 0 or 3. The scale default gives the same bytes as flags=bicubic.

The fourteen tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.
