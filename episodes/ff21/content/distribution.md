### Description

You set -g 100 in FFmpeg, expecting a keyframe every 100 frames, so every 4 seconds at 25 fps. Do you get one? We encode one synthetic clip four ways and read the keyframes two ways.

Source: 10 seconds at 25 fps (250 frames), 320x180, made by lavfi, with two hard cuts: at 3.2 s (smptebars to testsrc2) and at 6.8 s (to rgbtestsrc).

Readers: ffprobe packets flagged as keyframes, and ffprobe decoded frames whose picture type is I. They agree on every encode.

-g 100 alone: keyframes at 0, 3.2 and 6.8 s. They land on the cuts, not on a 4 s grid. The setting is a maximum, and libx264 adds a keyframe when the picture changes.

-g 100 -sc_threshold 0: keyframes at 0, 4 and 8 s. A strict 4 s grid, and the cuts get no keyframe. This is what segmented streaming (HLS, DASH) wants.

-force_key_frames 3.2,6.8 with scene detection off: exactly 0, 3.2, 6.8. -force_key_frames 2 with scene detection on: 0, 2, 3.2, 6.8, so the forced keyframe is added to the cuts.

The largest gap between keyframes never exceeded 4 s in any encode.

The seven tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.
