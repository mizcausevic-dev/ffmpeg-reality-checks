# FF64 distribution kit: FFmpeg -loop 1 -shortest: Picture Ends Early, 1 s of a 4.3 s Song

All numbers below come from the synthetic fixture in `episodes/ff64/fixture/` (experiment.mjs, run.mjs) and its tests, measured on ffmpeg 6.1.1. On 8.1.2 several results differ: `-fflags +shortest` is refused ("Unable to parse fflags option value shortest"), `-shortest_buf_duration 0.2` no longer leaves 25 fps alone (149 to 153 frames) and at 1 fps runs to the 20 s guard with only 0.683 s of audio, and a copied song at 1 fps gives 9 frames instead of 1.

### Description

You make a video from one still image and a song with -loop 1 -shortest. Is the video longer than the song? We build the video from a flat synthetic image and sine tones of 4.0, 4.3, 4.37 and 4.99 s at 25, 30, 24 and 1 frames per second, with the song re-encoded (pcm in mkv, aac in mp4) and copied (an aac file with -c:a copy), and measure the end of the picture and the end of the song.

Inputs (lavfi): a 96x72 flat PNG looped with -loop 1 -framerate N, and a 440 Hz sine at 48000 Hz written as 16 bit wav (192000, 206400, 209760 and 239520 samples) and once as aac in m4a (decodes to 206848 samples). Video is libx264 -tune stillimage, yuv420p. Every command also carries a hard -t, so nothing can run on.

Readers: ffprobe (video packet count, the end of the picture as the largest packet pts plus duration, the end of the song, the format duration) and a decode read in Node (framemd5 line count for the decoded frames, raw s16le sample count for the decoded audio). The packet count equals the decoded frame count in all 66 outputs.

Control: a 4.0 s song is a whole number of frames at 25, 30, 24 and 1 fps, so -shortest gives 100, 120, 96 and 4 frames, 4.000 s, exactly as long as the song.

With -shortest and the song re-encoded (pcm and aac) the picture is never longer than the song: it stops after floor(song length x frame rate) frames. At 25 fps a 4.3 s song gives 107 frames (4.28 s, 0.02 s short). At 1 fps the picture is 4 frames (4.0 s) for 4.3, 4.37 and 4.99 s songs: 0.30, 0.37 and 0.99 s short.

Copy the song with -c:a copy and -shortest, and the picture ends earlier: 105 frames (4.20 s) at 25 fps instead of 107, 126 at 30 fps instead of 129, 41 at 10 fps instead of 43, 5 at 2 fps instead of 8, and 1 frame (1.0 s) at 1 fps for a 4.3 s song, 3.3 s with no picture. The song is intact (4.300 s of packets). The cause is frames waiting inside the video encoder when the copied audio ends: -bf 0 gives 107, 129, 103, 43, 8 and 4 frames, and ffv1, which has no encoder delay, gives 107 at 25 fps and 4 at 1 fps.

When is the picture longer? With no stop option it runs to the -t guard: 200 frames (8.00 s) at 25 fps, 3.7 s too long. -fflags +shortest does not stop at the end of the song: 250 frames (10.00 s) at 25 fps and 300 at 30 fps. -t set to the song length rounds to the nearest frame: 4.3 s at 25 fps gives 108 frames, 4.32 s, 0.02 s longer, and 4.99 s gives 125 (5.00 s). -shortest_buf_duration 0.2 changes nothing at 25 fps, but at 1 fps the picture is 6 frames (6.00 s), 1.7 s longer than the song.

The 14 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1. The exact counts are asserted on 6.1.1 only; on 8.1.2 the tests assert the relations and the differences listed above.

Limits: one synthetic flat image, sine songs of four lengths, libx264 and ffv1, pcm, aac and copied aac, mkv and mp4, ffmpeg 6.1.1. How -shortest, -fflags +shortest and -shortest_buf_duration end an output changed between builds, so treat these counts as 6.1.1 numbers. Not tested: other encoders (x265, hardware), other containers, long songs, variable frame rate, real images and music, players (how they show a picture that ends before the audio), builds other than 6.1.1.
