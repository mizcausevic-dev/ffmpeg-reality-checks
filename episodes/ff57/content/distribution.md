### Description

You join two clips with xfade, and the crossfade starts at the wrong time, or the first clip is cut short. Where does the offset go? We crossfade two 5 second synthetic clips with xfade=transition=fade:duration=1 at five offsets, and classify every output frame as clip A, clip B or a blend.

Clips (lavfi, lossless x264, mkv, 96x72, 25 fps, yuv420p): two clips of 125 frames (5 s), every frame a flat card. Clip A frame n has luma 16+n and cb 96; clip B frame n has luma 235-n and cb 160. So one frame names its clip (from cb) and its frame number (from luma), and a blend has cb between 96 and 160. No frame hash is shared between the clips.

Readers: ffmpeg framemd5 (each output frame hash looked up in the hashes of the A and B frames) and raw decoded planes read in Node (mean luma, cb and cr of each frame, and whether the frame is flat). They name the same clip and the same frame number for every one of the 6 outputs.

Control: concat of A then B has 250 frames (10 s): A for 125 frames, then B for 125 frames, a hard cut, 0 blended frames. Known positive: offset 4 (clip A length 5 minus the fade 1) gives 225 frames, 9 s: clip A to 4.00 s, a blend for the next second (24 blended frames, the weight of B rising every frame, luma within 1 of the model of clip A frame i against clip B frame i minus 100), then pure B from 5.00 s, which is clip B frame 25 on, to clip B's last frame. No frame of clip A is lost.

Offset 3 starts the blend a second early: 200 frames, 8 s; the blend runs 3.00 s to 4.00 s, and the last 25 frames of clip A (1 s) never appear. Offset 2: 7 s, 2 s of A lost. Offset 1: 6 s, 3 s of A lost. The output is the offset plus 5 s, the blend is 24 frames every time, and only its position moves.

Offset 5, equal to the length of clip A, is the other failure: 126 frames (5.04 s), 125 frames of clip A, 0 blended frames, and a single frame of clip B (its last, frame 124). The crossfade never happens.

The rule: offset = the first clip's length minus the fade (here 5 - 1 = 4).

The 13 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1. They also pass on 8.1.2, but there the build-dependent claims (exact counts, bytes, error text) are not asserted, so the film's numbers are 6.1.1 numbers.
