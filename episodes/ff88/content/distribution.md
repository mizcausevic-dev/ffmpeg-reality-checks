### Description

You key a green screen with chromakey=0x00FF00 and nothing happens, or colorkey leaves a green fringe. What does the similarity number measure, how many edge pixels survive, and what does blend do? We key synthetic cards and read the alpha two ways.

Bench (no real footage): four 96x72 cards written by Node as raw rgb24. A probe card of 24 flat blocks (the key green 14B43C, the same green moved by +16, +40, -16 and -20 on every channel, darker greens, other hues, subject colors), a soft card (green with an orange disc whose edge mixes from green to orange over 10 pixels: 3888 green, 1628 edge and 1396 subject pixels, so the ideal matte is known per pixel), a noisy card (the green with up to 24 levels of noise per channel, an orange and a lime subject of 600 pixels each), and the key green alone. Filters: colorkey on rgba, chromakey on yuva444p, chromakey on yuva420p, chromakey with the key given in yuv (yuv=1). 4 blends x 11 similarities each, 176 runs.

Readers: the first is alphaextract to raw gray; the second is the same output as a PNG decoded in Node (zlib and unfiltering written for the test). They agree in all 176 runs. Formulas in Node: colorkey is the RGB distance divided by 255 times the square root of 3 (442), within 1 level of the filter in all 44 runs; chromakey is the mean over the 3x3 neighborhood of the U,V distance divided by 255 times the square root of 2 (361), exact on the interior in all 88 runs of the 4:4:4 variants.

Similarity: chromakey ignores brightness. The key and the key moved by +16, +40, -16 and -20 on every channel are all keyed first at similarity 0.03. Colorkey needs 0.01, 0.075, 0.2, 0.075 and 0.1 for the same five.

The key is not at zero (ffmpeg 6.1.1): the key color is converted with a full range matrix and the pixels are limited range. For 14B43C the key is U,V 95,58 and the card 99,66: 6 levels of alpha, 0.0248. A card of exactly the key color keeps alpha 255 at the default similarity 0.01 and 0.02 and is keyed from 0.03. Over six key colors the distance is 6, 11, 6, 10, 1 and 0 levels (the model gets each within 1); four of the six cards stay whole at 0.01. With yuv=1 and the pixel's own YUV the distance is 0 for all six. Colorkey is at distance 0 for all six at every similarity.

Soft edge: 1628 edge pixels. With blend 0 there are two alpha levels. At similarity 0.1, colorkey keeps 1236 edge pixels fully opaque and 504 of them are less than half subject; chromakey keeps 1116 and 384. Blend 0.1 gives 29 levels (colorkey) and 60 (chromakey). With similarity near 0 and blend 0.4883 (the subject's distance, 124/255 plus 0.002), colorkey follows the true matte: 127 levels, mean error 0.53 of 255, nothing of the background kept. Chromakey with its matched blend 0.3118: mean error 10.8 and all 3888 background pixels keep some alpha.

Noise: at the defaults colorkey leaves 5699 of 5712 background pixels opaque and chromakey all 5712. Across the 44 settings of the sweep colorkey has 22 clean ones (no opaque speck, both subjects whole), chromakey has none: at similarity 0.1 and blend 0, 69 specks; at 0.15, none, and 52 lime pixels lost.

The 17 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1.

The default similarity result, the key offset (U,V 95,58 against 99,66), the 22 clean colorkey settings, the 3x3 border behavior (the outer ring of pixels is left out of the chromakey comparison) and the exact edge counts are the parts most likely to differ on another build. Not tested: real footage, compression noise, other key colors and noise levels, hardware filters, the despill filters.
