# FF30 distribution kit: FFmpeg 6.1.1 VP9: -b:v 0 Changes Nothing, an Explicit -b:v Caps It

### Description

The advice for VP9 is everywhere: -crf needs -b:v 0, or ffmpeg caps the bitrate and the video turns blocky. Is that true on this build? We encode a noisy synthetic clip with libvpx-vp9 ten ways, change only -crf and -b:v, and measure the files.

Clip (lavfi testsrc2 plus deterministic noise on luma, 320x180, 25 fps, 50 frames, 2 s, stored lossless in FFV1). Every encode: libvpx-vp9 -deadline good -cpu-used 5 -row-mt 1.

Readers. Bitrate: ffprobe packet sizes (not the container header), and a second count from copying the packets into an IVF file and summing the frame sizes in Node. They match on every encode. Quality against the lossless clip: the ffmpeg psnr filter, and our own PSNR from decoded raw frames in Node (all planes, same definition). They agree within 0.02 dB on every encode. SSIM comes from the ffmpeg ssim filter only (one reader), and is reported as a supporting number.

-crf 30 alone: 2829 kbps, PSNR 35.3 dB, SSIM 0.949. -crf 30 -b:v 0: the same file. The video packets are byte for byte identical (same packet md5 from ffmpeg and from Node, same byte count) and the decoded frames are identical. -crf 20 and -crf 40 also match their -b:v 0 twins (4194 and 960 kbps). On this build there is no hidden cap: -crf alone runs far above 256 kbps.

A cap appears only when you write one. -crf 30 -b:v 256k: 457 kbps, PSNR 28.2 dB, SSIM 0.655. -crf 30 -b:v 500k: 697 kbps, PSNR 28.7 dB, SSIM 0.676. At -crf 20, adding -b:v 500k drops PSNR from 40.2 to 28.8 dB (11.5 dB lost). The cap is a target, not a wall: all three -b:v 500k encodes came out above 500 kbps on this noisy clip.

Under the cap, crf almost stops mattering. With -b:v 500k: -crf 20 gives 726 kbps and 28.8 dB, -crf 40 gives 665 kbps and 28.7 dB. Different files, same quality. Control, the same crf values with -b:v 0: 4194 kbps and 40.2 dB against 960 kbps and 29.6 dB.

What to check: look for a stray -b:v in the command, a preset, or a wrapper. -b:v 0 is harmless on this build but made no difference here.

The ten tests pass three times in a row on ffmpeg 6.1.1 (ffmpeg version 6.1.1-3ubuntu5).
