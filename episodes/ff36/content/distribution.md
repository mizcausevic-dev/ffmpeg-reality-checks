### Description

You set -maxrate 2M on a libx264 encode and the bitrate comes out higher. Why? On its own -maxrate does nothing: libx264 needs -bufsize too. We measure the average bitrate, the busiest second, and the quality cost.

Source (lavfi, lossless FFV1): 8 s of testsrc2 at 320x180, 25 fps, 200 frames, with deterministic noise added so it is hard to compress. Every encode is libx264 -preset medium -threads 1. Baseline -crf 18 alone: 3588 kbps average, busiest second 3676 kbps, PSNR 37.80 dB.

Readers: ffprobe packet sizes in a sliding 1 s window, and ffmpeg -c copy to a raw H.264 stream where Node sums the frame sizes. They agree within 1 percent. Identity by ffmpeg packet md5 and by a Node md5 of the raw stream. PSNR by the ffmpeg psnr filter and by Node.

-crf 18 -maxrate 2M with no -bufsize: ffmpeg prints "VBV maxrate specified, but no bufsize, ignored". The output is the same bitstream as -crf 18 alone: same packet md5, 3588 kbps, busiest second 3676 kbps, PSNR 37.80 dB. -maxrate 2000k and -x264-params crf=18:vbv-maxrate=2000 give the same bytes, and -bufsize 2M without -maxrate is ignored too.

-crf 18 -maxrate 2M -bufsize 2M: 2098 kbps average, busiest second 2593 kbps (still above 2000), PSNR 32.05 dB. With -bufsize 4M: 2198 kbps, busiest second 2942 kbps, PSNR 32.36 dB.

Control: -bufsize 200k puts the busiest second at 2077 kbps, so the window readers can see a cap. Average mode -b:v 2M gives 2024 kbps average, but the busiest second is 2531 kbps and PSNR is 31.73 dB.

x264 core 164 r3108 (libx264 in ffmpeg 6.1.1) and x264 core 165 r3223 (ffmpeg 8.1.2). The kilobit numbers above are from 6.1.1; the relations hold on both. The tests assert relations with margins, not exact bitrates. The thirteen tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.
