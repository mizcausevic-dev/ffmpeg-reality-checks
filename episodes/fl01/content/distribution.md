### Description

You ask FFmpeg for 3 seconds starting at 7.5 s, and add -c copy so it is instant. Depending on where you type one flag, you get 75 frames or 13. This deep dive re-measures five earlier checks on synthetic clips, adds one new one, and ends with a command you can trust.

Readers: frame md5 matched back to the source, and a second tool (ffprobe packets, or a frame number stored in the pixels). Each has a control that has to fail on a wrong answer.

Which seconds (20 s clip, 500 unique frames, lossless). -ss 5 -to 10 before -i: 5 s, 125 frames, source 5 to 10 s. -ss 5 -i ... -to 10: 10 s, 250 frames, source 5 to 15 s, because the seek restarts the output clock. -ss 5 -i ... -t 5: 5 s.

Where -c copy starts (keyframes at 0, 5, 10 and 15 s, 3 s from 7.5 s). -ss before -i: starts at 7.52 s with 75 frames, but the file holds 138 packets, 63 of them hidden pre-roll. -ss after -i: starts at 10 s with 13 frames (0.52 s). Re-encode: frame 188, 74 frames.

Who places keyframes (10 s clip, hard cuts at 3.2 s and 6.8 s). -g 100 alone: 0, 3.2, 6.8 s, a maximum. Add -sc_threshold 0: 0, 4, 8 s. -force_key_frames 3.2,6.8: exactly those.

From the end (10 s at 30 fps, 300 frames). -sseof -3 re-encoded: 90 frames, 210 to 299. With -c copy and -g 100: 100 frames, 3.333 s, starting at keyframe 200. With -g 10: 90. At -sseof -2.9 the re-encode gives 87 frames and the copy 90. A 10 s picture with 12 s of audio: 1 s of picture (30 frames) and 3 s of audio. -sseof -30 on a 10 s file: all 300 frames.

The other end (5 s picture, 125 frames, 3 s tone). -shortest -c copy: the picture stops at 73 of 125 frames. Re-encode: 75. -t 3 -c copy: 77 frames, 3.08 s. With an 8 s tone the file runs 8 s without -shortest and 5 s with it.

The recipe (new: a fresh 20 s clip at 30 fps with 600 numbered frames, 3 s from 7.5 s is frames 225 to 314). -ss 7.5 -i in.mp4 -t 3, re-encoded: exactly 90 frames, both readers agree. Control, -i in.mp4 -ss 7.5 -t 3 -c copy: 15 frames starting at frame 300, flagged by both readers. -ss before -i with -c copy: the right 90 frames, but 165 packets, 75 of them hidden. Keyframes forced at 7.5 s and 10.5 s: the copy gives 90 frames and 90 packets, with -ss on either side of -i.

The tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 (three runs in a row).
