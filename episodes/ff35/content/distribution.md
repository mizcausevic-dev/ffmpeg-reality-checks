### Description

You set -hls_time 3 and the playlist comes back with segments of 4 s and 2 s. Not one is 3 s. Why? The HLS muxer cuts only at keyframes. We cut a synthetic clip and measure every segment.

Source (lavfi testsrc2, libx264): 20 s at 25 fps, 500 frames of 64x64, a fixed 50 frame GOP (-g 50), so keyframes at 0, 2, 4 ... 18 s. Control: a 75 frame GOP, equal to the ask in frames.

Readers: the playlist EXTINF value of each segment, ffprobe packet timestamps inside each .ts file, and a decode of each .ts on its own (framemd5). They agree on every segment of every keyframe-cut output.

-f hls -hls_time 3 with the 2 s GOP: EXTINF 4 2 4 2 4 2 2, TARGETDURATION 4, 7 segments, 0 of 7 are 3 s. Cuts at 0, 4, 6, 10, 12, 16, 18 s, every one a source keyframe. The segments hold 500 of the 500 frames and add up to 20 s: nothing is lost, the lengths are just not yours.

The rule that predicted every cut: the first keyframe after the last cut and at or after n times the ask. -hls_time 5 gives 6 4 6 4 (TARGETDURATION 6). -hls_time 1 gives ten segments of 2 s.

-force_key_frames "expr:gte(t,n_forced*3)" gives 3 3 3 3 3 3 2 (TARGETDURATION 3), every segment starting on a keyframe. The control with -g 75 gives the same 3 3 3 3 3 3 2. -c copy keeps the source keyframes and gives 4 2 4 2 4 2 2 again.

-hls_flags split_by_time cuts near 3 s, but 3 of the 7 segments start off a keyframe and lose frames when decoded alone.

The thirteen tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1.
