### Description

The option -r sets a frame rate. Put it before the input and it means one thing; after, another. What happens? We build a 2 second clip at 30 fps (60 frames) and ask for 10 fps both ways.

Readers: ffprobe (declared duration and frame rate) and a look inside (packets in the container, frames a full decode produces, gaps between timestamps).

-r 10 after the input drops frames: 10 fps, 22 frames, 2.2 s. The fps=10 filter is exact: 20 frames, 2.0 s.

-r 10 before the input keeps all 60 frames and relabels the clip as 10 fps: it plays for 6 s, in slow motion at one third speed. -r 10 on both sides gives the same 6 s.

With -c copy, -r before the input has no effect on an MP4: still 30 fps, 2 s.

On a raw H.264 stream, which stores no timing, -r before the input is how you say the rate: 30 fps by default, 10 fps (6 s) with -r 10.

Same results on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.
