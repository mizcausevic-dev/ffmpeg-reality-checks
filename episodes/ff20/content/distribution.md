# FF20 distribution kit: FFmpeg's Default AAC Bitrate: 128 kbps Stereo, 69 kbps Mono

### Description

You encode audio with FFmpeg's native AAC encoder and set no bitrate. What do you get? Does it depend on the channels or the sample rate? We encode synthetic pink noise at four sample rates (22.05, 32, 44.1 and 48 kHz), in mono and stereo, and read each file two ways.

Source: 10 seconds of pink noise from lavfi anoisesrc, with a different seed in each channel, and 10 seconds of digital silence as a control.

Readers: ffprobe (the bit_rate the stream declares) and the sum of every packet's payload size, times 8, over the duration.

Stereo with no -b:a: about 128 kbps (127.2 to 128.1) at all four sample rates. Mono: about 69 kbps (69.0 to 69.4). Not 64, and not half of stereo: it is 0.54 of it. Both readers agree within 1%.

Digital silence with no -b:a costs about 2.1 kbps: the default is a target the encoder only fills when there is sound to code.

Control: libmp3lame writes a fixed 128 kbps for stereo and 64 kbps for mono. An explicit -b:a 96k gives 95.9 kbps (stereo) and 96.3 kbps (mono).

The six tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2 with the same numbers.
