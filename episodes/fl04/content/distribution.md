### Description

Speed a clip up two times, loop it three times, keep every frame, and you can still end up with 450 distinct timestamps for 900 frames. This deep dive follows one question through five FFmpeg Reality Checks: when I change the frame rate, the speed or the length, how many frames and how many seconds do I really get?

Every clip is synthetic (lavfi) and every frame carries its own code. Reader one is ffprobe (packet timestamps and durations). Reader two is a decode (frame md5, decoded frame code, decoded timestamps, sample count, pitch). Each result has a known-positive control. All numbers below were measured on ffmpeg 6.1.1.

1. Variable to constant frame rate (episode 5): a 5 s clip with 93 frames and gaps of 33, 67 and 933 ms. With no rate option, 6.1.1 writes 150 frames, 57 of them duplicates. -fps_mode passthrough keeps 93, -fps_mode cfr -r 30 gives 150, and there are 93 distinct pictures in every variant. On 8.1.2 (published result) the default keeps 93.

2. -r before or after -i (episode 16): 60 frames at 30 fps. -r 10 after -i gives 22 frames and 2.2 s, the fps=10 filter gives 20 frames and 2.0 s, and -r 10 before -i keeps 60 frames and plays 6 s. With -c copy it has no effect.

3. Which frame fps keeps (episode 25): at 25 fps, fps=1 keeps frames 12, 37, 62, the first at 0.48 s, and round=up keeps 0, 25, 50. Converting 60 fps to 24 fps never uses frame 0.

4. setpts and the audio (episode 29): setpts=0.5*PTS gives 5.07 s of picture and still 10.0 s of audio. At the default 30 fps only 152 of 300 frames survive. atempo=2 gives 5 s of sound at 440 Hz, asetrate gives 880 Hz.

5. -stream_loop (episode 33): with picture and audio both 4 s, -stream_loop 2 gives 360 frames in 12 s. With audio 3.9 s, re-encoded, 6.1.1 keeps 355 frames (11.83 s) and 8.1.2 keeps 359 (published result). With audio 4.1 s the loop is 12.3 s, with picture holes of 0.13 s.

New in this film, the recipe:
ffmpeg -stream_loop 2 -i in.mkv -vf setpts=0.5*PTS,fps=60 -af atempo=2 out.mkv
It gives 900 frames in source order and 900 distinct timestamps (gaps of 16 to 17 ms), with picture, sound and container about 15 s and the pitch at 440 Hz, on both readers. The controls: -fps_mode passthrough keeps 900 frames but only 450 distinct timestamps; -r 60 after -i gives 901 frames with frame 0 shown twice; without atempo the sound is 30 s under 15 s of picture; with no output rate only 452 frames are kept.
