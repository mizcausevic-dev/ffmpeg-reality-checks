### Description

A GIF can hold 256 colors at most. FFmpeg's plain command makes one anyway, and it often looks muddy. How much worse is it, and what does a palette step cost? We make two synthetic clips and measure.

The clips: 320x180, 20 frames. One is a smooth gradient (a fractal) with 5,299 colors; the other is flat shapes and text with 15,124. We compare each GIF with its lossless source three ways: PSNR, SSIM, and the number of colors the GIF really uses.

Plain command (ffmpeg -i in.mkv out.gif): PSNR 21.6 dB (gradient) and 28.7 dB (shapes). SSIM 0.40 and 0.84. 207 and 202 colors used.

With palettegen and paletteuse: PSNR 35.3 and 39.7 dB. SSIM 0.97 and 0.98. 253 and 255 colors.

File size: on the gradient the palette file is smaller (about 460 kB against 584 kB). On the flat shapes it is larger (about 120 kB against 104 kB). Sizes differ by under 1 percent between ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.

With paletteuse=dither=none the scores rise again (36.2 and 40.7 dB) and the files shrink (about 390 kB and 103 kB). The scores do not measure how banding looks to the eye; we did not test that.

Control: a 4 color palette scores 13.8 and 9.7 dB, so the scores can fall.
