# FF58 distribution kit: FFmpeg -ss With -c copy: 13 Extra Frames Before -i, a 0.48 s Gap After

### Description

You cut a clip with -ss and -c copy, and the cut starts wrong: extra video at the front, or an empty start. Where does a stream copy cut really start? We cut a synthetic 4 second clip with a keyframe every second, at four times, with -ss before -i and after -i, into mp4 and mkv, and measure the first frame, the frames you did not ask for and the frames missing.

Clip (lavfi, lossless x264, mkv, 96x72, 25 fps, yuv420p): 100 frames (4 s), no B-frames, keyframes at frames 0, 25, 50 and 75 (0, 1, 2 and 3 s). Frame N is a flat card of luma 16 + 2 N, so a decoded frame names its own source frame. The cuts ask for -ss 0.48, 1.0, 1.52 and 1.96 (frames 12, 25, 38 and 49), 16 stream copy files, plus two controls.

Readers: ffprobe (first packet and first frame times) with ffmpeg framemd5 matched against the source frame hashes; and Node on raw decoded luma, on the mp4 edit list (elst box) and on the mkv first block time. They name the same source frame for every frame of all 18 files.

Control: -ss 1.0, exactly on a keyframe, gives 75 frames, source frames 25 to 99, nothing extra, nothing missing, first frame at 0.00 s, in all four copies (before or after -i, mp4 and mkv). A re-encode with -ss 1.52 (accurate seek) gives 62 frames, source 38 to 99, no gap.

-ss 1.52 before -i, mkv: the cut starts at the keyframe before, frame 25, so 13 frames you did not ask for lead it (0.52 s), the first frame is at 0.00 s and 75 frames play. At -ss 1.96: 24 extra frames. At -ss 0.48: 12.

-ss 1.52 before -i, mp4: the file stores the same 75 packets, but its edit list skips the first 0.52 s (media time 8320 of 16000 per second) and ffmpeg plays 62 frames, from frame 38. Whether a player honors the edit list was not tested.

-ss 1.52 after -i, mp4 and mkv: the cut starts at the keyframe after, frame 50, with its first frame at 0.48 s and nothing before it (an empty mp4 edit of 0.48 s, an mkv first block at 0.48 s), and 12 frames you asked for are missing; 50 frames play. At -ss 1.96: 0.04 s and 1 frame. At -ss 0.48: 0.52 s and 13 frames. A player may show that empty start as black; players were not tested.

The rule: for a time between keyframes, the extra frames before -i and the missing frames after -i always add up to 25 frames (1 s). On a keyframe both are 0.

The 12 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1.

Limits: one synthetic lossless clip, x264 without B-frames, a keyframe every 25 frames, stream copy into mp4 and mkv, ffmpeg 6.1.1. The mp4 edit list handling and the decode timing are the parts most likely to differ between builds. Not tested: audio, B-frames, players (whether they show the empty start as black or ignore the edit list), real footage, other codecs, builds other than 6.1.1.
