### Description

You turn a stereo file into mono with FFmpeg: -ac 1. Is the mono file as loud as the stereo one? We make four synthetic stereo files and turn each into mono three ways.

Sources (2 s, 48 kHz, 16-bit, made by lavfi): the left channel is a 440 Hz sine at -7.96 dBFS. The right channel is (a) identical, (b) inverted, (c) silent, or (d) a different tone (660 Hz).

Readers: ffmpeg astats (peak and RMS) and the raw 16-bit samples, with peak and RMS computed in JavaScript.

-ac 1 averages the channels. Identical channels: no change (peak -7.96 dB). Only the left channel has sound: the peak falls 6.02 dB, to -13.98 dB. Right channel inverted: the average is zero, and both readers see digital silence (minus infinity). Different tones: RMS falls 3 dB against one channel.

-af pan=mono|c0=c0 keeps the left channel at its own level for all four sources. -af pan=mono|c0=c0+c1 adds the channels: identical channels gain 6 dB (peak -1.94 dB), and inverted channels still cancel.

The right mix depends on the recording. Check loudness and phase before you trust a mono file.
