### Description

The FFmpeg thumbnail filter promises "the most representative frame". Which frame is that, and is it the best one? We build a 100 frame synthetic clip, a dark title card followed by a colourful scene, run the filter with several batch sizes, and read which frame it picked four independent ways.

Clip (lavfi, lossless ffv1, 160x120, 25 fps): frames 0 to 54 are a dark title card with a white loading bar that grows 3 pixels a frame, frames 55 to 99 are testsrc2 (colour bars, a frame counter, motion). All 100 frames are different. Every filter chain starts with format=rgb24 because the filter builds RGB histograms.

Readers of which frame: ffmpeg framemd5 (the timestamp gives the frame index, the hash is looked up in the source's hash list), the raw rgb24 bytes of the output matched byte for byte against every source frame in Node, and the filter's algorithm rewritten in Node (an RGB histogram per frame, the average of the batch, the smallest sum of squared differences wins, the first wins a tie). Readers of how much is in a picture: luma entropy (Node on the decoded gray plane and ffmpeg's entropy filter, within 0.002 bits on all 100 frames) and mean luma (Node and signalstats YAVG, within 0.05). All readers agree on all 10 cases.

Controls: a ramp of 99 frames (a white bar one pixel wider each frame) has the average histogram of frame 49, and thumbnail=n=99 picks 49. A clip of 100 identical dark frames with one white frame 37: thumbnail never picks 37, it picks frame 0.

Results: thumbnail with no options is thumbnail=n=100, and it picks frame 54, the last frame of the title card: mean luma 39, luma entropy 0.44 bits. The most detailed frame is 80: mean luma 114, entropy 4.55 bits, ten times more. Frame 54 ranks 46 of 100 by entropy: every scene frame beats it. It picks one frame per batch of n: n=50 gives frames 24 (title card) and 93 (scene), n=25 gives 12, 37, 67, 86, n=10 gives ten frames, n=30 gives 14, 54, 74, 96 (the last batch is a partial 10), and a 60 frame clip with the default n still gets one frame, at the end. Making the batch the scene (trim=start_frame=55,thumbnail=n=45) picks frame 74: mean luma 114, entropy 4.45 bits (98 percent of the best), rank 32 of 100. The algorithm in Node, using colour histograms only, predicted the pick in all 10 cases.

The 13 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.
