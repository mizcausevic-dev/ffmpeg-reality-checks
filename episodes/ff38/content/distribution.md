### Description

Your video looks stretched or squished after scaling. What are SAR and DAR, and what does setsar do? SAR is the shape of one pixel, DAR is the shape of the picture, and displayed width is stored width x SAR. We measure what scale and setsar do to them.

Source (lavfi, lossless x264, mp4): a 720x576 test card with SAR 64:45 (DAR 16:9). Its white shape is an ellipse 269x383 pixels (width/height 0.70); widened by SAR (720 x 64/45 = 1024 wide) it is a round circle. Control: a 640x360 square-pixel card (SAR 1:1) with a 199x199 circle.

Three readers: ffprobe (width, height, sample_aspect_ratio, display_aspect_ratio), raw decoded pixels (the shape's measured width and height), and the mp4 pasp box parsed from the file bytes. Ratios are compared by cross-multiplication.

-vf scale=1280:720 does not carry the SAR over: the output is 1280x720, SAR 1:1, DAR 16:9, and the pixels are a circle (478x479). The control agrees: the square card scaled to 640x720 gets SAR 2:1, pixels 199x398, DAR still 16:9. scale keeps the DAR and rewrites the SAR. -s 1280x720 gives the same pixels.

-vf setsar=1 changes only the flag: 720x576, SAR 1:1, DAR 5:4, identical pixel hash, and a player that honors SAR would show the circle 0.70 wide. -vf scale=iw*sar:ih,setsar=1 bakes the aspect into the pixels: 1024x576, SAR 1:1, DAR 16:9, circle 383x383. scale=iw*sar:ih alone gives the same.

Order matters: -vf setsar=1,scale=1280:720 has the same pixels as the plain scale (478x479) but the flag is SAR 45:64, DAR 5:4, so the displayed picture is 900x720 and the circle is 0.70 wide.

The eleven tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.
