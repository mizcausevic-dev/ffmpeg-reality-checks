# FF55 distribution kit: FFmpeg 29.97 fps, -video_track_timescale 1000: Frames Are 33 or 34 ms

### Description

A 29.97 fps frame lasts 33.37 ms, which is not a whole number, and an mp4 stores timestamps as whole ticks of the track timescale. Do the timestamps round? We write the same 300 frame synthetic clip with -video_track_timescale 30000, 90000, 24000, 15360, 1000, 600 and with no option, and measure the stored time of every frame.

Clip (lavfi color, 96x72, 300 frames at 30000/1001 fps, lossless x264 with -bf 0, mp4): the exact time of frame n is n x 1001 / 30000 seconds and the clip lasts 10.01 s. With no B-frames a frame time is its decode time. Controls: a 25 fps clip (250 frames, 40 ms each) at timescale 1000, and the clip at timescale 30000.

Readers: the mp4 file itself, parsed in Node (the mdhd timescale and duration and the stts table that holds the frame lengths in ticks, with a check that there is no ctts table), and ffprobe (the time_base, r_frame_rate, avg_frame_rate, the pts of every packet and of every decoded frame). They agree tick for tick on all 9 files. ffmpeg framemd5 counts the 300 frames in every file but prints their times as frame numbers 0 to 299, in frame rate units, so it cannot show this rounding.

Control: timescale 30000 stores 1001 ticks for every one of the 300 frames, timescale 90000 stores 3003, zero error on every frame, and the clip lasts exactly 10.010000 s. The 25 fps clip at timescale 1000 stores 40 ticks for each of its 250 frames, so rounding comes from the frame length, not from the timescale.

Timescale 1000: frames are 33 or 34 ms long, never 33.37. 190 frames are 33 ms and 110 are 34 ms. The stored times of the first frames are 0, 33, 67, 100, 133, 167. Each time is rounded from its exact value, so the error never goes above half a tick, 0.50 ms, and it does not accumulate: the last frame is 0.37 ms off and the clip still lasts 10.01 s. Timescale 600: frames of 20 ticks, and 6 of 300 of 21 ticks, up to 0.83 ms off. Timescale 24000: 800 or 801 ticks, up to 0.017 ms off. Timescale 15360: 512 or 513 ticks, up to 0.032 ms off, and ffprobe reads an average frame rate of 2304000/76877 instead of 30000/1001, and a duration of 10.010026 s. Every stored tick equals round(n x 1001 x T / 30000), halves up (frame 15 at timescale 1000 is exactly 500.5 ticks and is stored as 501). Only timescales that are multiples of 30000 are exact. With no option the timescale was 30000 on this build.

29.97 typed as a decimal is not 30000/1001. A lavfi source made with r=29.97 is read by ffprobe as 2997/100, gets timescale 11988 by default with 400 ticks per frame, lasts 10.01001 s, and is 0.010 ms behind the 30000/1001 grid at its last frame.

The 14 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1. They also pass on 8.1.2, but there the build-dependent claims (exact counts, bytes, error text) are not asserted, so the film's numbers are 6.1.1 numbers.
