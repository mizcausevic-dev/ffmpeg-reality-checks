### Description

You ask FFmpeg for 3 seconds from 7.5 s with -c copy and get 13 frames, half a second. Why, and what to type instead.

Synthetic clips, read two ways (frame md5 matched to the source, and ffprobe or a frame number in the pixels). -ss before -i with -c copy: starts at 7.52 s, 75 frames, but 138 packets, 63 hidden. -ss after -i: starts at 10 s, 13 frames. Re-encode: frame 188, 74 frames.

-g 100 is a maximum: keyframes at 0, 3.2, 6.8 s on the scene cuts; with -sc_threshold 0, at 0, 4, 8 s; -force_key_frames 3.2,6.8 puts them exactly there.

Fresh clip, 3 s from 7.5 s is frames 225 to 314 (90 frames). -ss 7.5 -i in.mp4 -t 3 re-encoded: exactly those. Control, -ss after -i with -c copy: 15 frames from frame 300. A plain copy carries 165 packets, 75 hidden. Keyframes forced at 7.5 s and 10.5 s: 90 frames and 90 packets.

From the end: -sseof -3 gives 90 frames re-encoded, 100 with -c copy and -g 100. -shortest -c copy: 73 of 125 frames, -t 3 -c copy: 77, re-encode: 75.

Measured on ffmpeg 6.1.1. Not tested: real footage, players, other encoders. The full film is on this channel: "FFmpeg Cut Video: Where -ss, -to and -c copy Really Start and End".
