### Description

You cut a clip with -ss 3.5 and add -copyts to keep the timestamps. What is kept, and which frame comes first? We cut one synthetic clip in three containers and read the result two ways.

Bench (no real footage): a 64x48 clip of 100 frames at 10 fps, a keyframe every 1.0 s, no B frames, lossless libx264. Node writes the raw frames so that frame n has luma 16 + 2n: a decoded picture names itself. Containers: mp4, mkv, ts (the mpegts muxer starts its clock at 1.4 s), plus two controls that move the start time without changing the container: an mkv shifted to start at 1.4 s (-output_ts_offset 1.4) and a ts that starts at 0 (-muxdelay 0 -muxpreload 0).

Readers: ffprobe (format and stream start_time, the pts_time of every packet) and the decoded pictures read in Node (which frame is first, how many, contiguous), with the decoder's own first timestamp from showinfo. They agree on the first timestamp of every re-encoded cut (48 cells: 30 re-encoded, 18 stream copy).

Without -copyts (ffmpeg 6.1.1): -ss restarts the clock. mp4 and mkv start at 0 with frame 35 and 65 frames, whether -ss is before or after -i. ts starts at 1.4 because its muxer adds that.

-copyts with -ss before -i keeps the source clock: mp4 and mkv start at 3.5, the time frame 35 had in the source. In ts the muxer adds 1.4 on top: frame 40 at 6.8 s, not the 5.4 s it had in the source. -start_at_zero gives 5.4.

-copyts with -ss after -i measures the cut on the file's own clock. A file that starts at 1.4 s (the ts, and the mkv shifted to 1.4) is cut 1.4 s early: 79 frames from frame 21 instead of 65 from frame 35. A file that starts at 0 (mp4, mkv, ts from 0) is not affected. -start_at_zero brings frame 35 back.

Which frame is first with -ss before -i: re-encoded, mp4 and mkv land exactly (frame = 10 x seconds). The ts demuxer lands on a keyframe at or after the request even when re-encoding: -ss 3.2 starts at frame 40, not 32. Stream copy in mkv starts at the keyframe before (frame 30 for 3.2 s). Stream copy in mp4 has an edit list: 70 packets, 65 pictures, and the first packet at -0.5 s while ffprobe start_time says 0.

The 11 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1.
