### Description

You add itsoffset 1 to the audio input and copy the streams, no re-encode. Does the audio really move, in MP4 and in MKV? We build a four second synthetic clip, run five cases in both containers, and read the start times with three independent readers.

Clip (lavfi): 100 different frames (96x72, 25 fps, lossless) and 192000 samples of mono 48000 Hz ALAC (no encoder delay): digital silence, then a 1000 Hz tone starting at 1.500 s. Audio and picture are two inputs, copied with -c copy into .mp4 and .mkv.

Readers: ffprobe (stream start times and the decoded tone position), Node on the container bytes (mp4 edit lists and sample tables, mkv cluster and block times), and ffmpeg with -ignore_editlist 1 (mp4 only). They agree on all 10 files.

Controls with known answers: with no offset, picture and audio start at 0.000 s and the tone is at 1.500 s in both containers. A real delay (adelay=1000, audio re-encoded to ALAC) moves the tone to 2.500 s (240000 samples) while the container start times stay 0.000 s: the decoded reader sees what the stamps cannot.

Results: -itsoffset 1 on the audio moves it by exactly 1.000 s in both containers: audio start 1.000 s, tone 2.500 s, picture 0.000 s, all 192000 samples equal to the source. In the mp4 that second is an empty edit of 1000 ms in the audio edit list; in the mkv it is the first audio block at 1.000 s. With -ignore_editlist 1 the mp4 audio starts at 0.000 s again, tone at 1.500 s, same hash as the file with no offset. -itsoffset -1 on the audio is -1.000 s against the picture in both, but built differently: the mp4 keeps the picture at 0.000 s and its edit list skips the first 48000 samples (144000 decode, equal to the source tail, tone at 0.500 s); the mkv keeps all 192000 samples and starts the picture at 1.000 s. -itsoffset 1 on the video input gives the same -1.000 s the other way: picture 1.000 s, audio 0.000 s. In all 10 files the 100 frames are the source frames and no audio sample was changed by copy.

The 12 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 (8.1.2 pending).
