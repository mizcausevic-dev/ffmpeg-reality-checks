### Description

Should you add -vf yadif to every video, just in case? On interlaced footage it removes combing. What does it do to a clip that is already progressive? We measure frames changed, whether one-pixel row detail survives, and what happens to the frame count.

Clips (lavfi, lossless x264, mp4, 320x240, 50 frames each): fine is progressive with a patch of one-pixel stripes, a patch of static one-pixel lines and a moving striped box; smooth is progressive with no fine row detail; the control is truly interlaced, woven from two moments with the interlace filter. Combing is measured as the mean absolute difference between adjacent rows over the difference between rows two apart: 5.5 on the woven control, 0.5 on the smooth progressive clip.

Readers: raw decoded luma planes read in Node (frame compare, row difference, PSNR), ffmpeg framemd5 (one hash per decoded frame, with timestamps), and ffmpeg idet (counts of progressive and interlaced frames, from stderr). Raw luma and framemd5 agree on every case.

-vf yadif with no options on the fine progressive clip changes 50 of 50 frames. In the stripe patch the adjacent-row difference falls from 180 to 1.6 (PSNR against the source 6.1 dB), the moving striped box from 38.5 to 0.1, and the whole-frame PSNR is 10.1 dB. Static single-pixel lines come through identical (87.1 before and after). Frame count (50) and timestamps (0.04 s apart) do not change. On the smooth clip yadif still changes 50 of 50 frames, but about 14 pixels each, 52.7 dB.

-vf yadif=deint=interlaced changes 0 of 50 frames of a clip flagged progressive: the same frame md5s as the source. But it follows the flag, not the picture. The same fine pixels encoded with -flags +ilme+ildct (ffprobe field_order no longer progressive) change 50 of 50 frames and lose the stripes (180 to 1.5), and setfield=tff in front of it does the same.

Control: on the woven clip yadif takes combing from 5.5 to 0.5 and idet goes from 50 interlaced frames to 50 progressive. yadif=mode=1 (send_field) doubles the frame rate: 50 frames in, 100 out, a timestamp every 0.02 s. idet itself cannot call the fine clip progressive (50 undetermined).

The sixteen tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.
