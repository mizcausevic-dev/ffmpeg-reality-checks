### Description

ffprobe prints a format duration, a stream duration and, if you add up the packets, a third. They do not always agree. Which one is true? We build 12 synthetic files and read every duration three ways.

Files (lavfi, 12 files): video is 100 frames at 25 fps (4 s, 96x72, lossless x264, copied). Audio is a 440 Hz sine at 48000 Hz mono: pcm_s16le in mkv and mov, aac in mp4 and ts. Three conditions per container: same (audio 4 s, starts with the video), long (audio 5 s) and late (audio 4 s, -itsoffset 1, so it starts 1 s after the video).

Readers: ffprobe (format.duration and start_time, stream start_time and duration, the DURATION tag, every packet pts_time and duration_time); the decoder (ffmpeg decodes to raw frames and raw s16le samples, Node counts them); and the container bytes read in Node (mp4 and mov boxes mvhd, tkhd, mdhd, elst and stts; the matroska Info Duration, DURATION tags and block times; the transport stream PES time stamps).

Control: a mov with 4 s of video and 4 s of audio. Every number says 4.0 s: format, both stream durations, packet spans, the decoder (100 frames, 192000 samples), and the bytes (mvhd holds 4000 ticks at timescale 1000).

Audio 5 s (mkv, mov, mp4): format.duration is 5.0 s while the video stream says 4.0 s, and both are true. The format duration is the latest stream end minus the earliest stream start (it matches that model within 2 ms in every mov, mp4 and ts file; in mkv it equals the Info Duration, 5000 ms stored as an 8 byte float).

Audio 4 s starting at 1 s (mkv, mov): format 5.0 s, though no stream holds more than 4 s. The mov edit list has an empty edit of 1000 ticks (media time -1); the mkv first audio block sits at 1.000 s. The same late audio gets two meanings of stream duration: the mov says 4.0 s (its length), ffprobe has no duration field for the mkv stream and its DURATION tag says 5.0 s (its end time).

Packets: in mkv the sum of the audio packet durations is 3.938 s for 4.000 s of audio (4.922 s for 5.000 s). Block times are whole milliseconds with steps of 42 and 43 ms, and each 2048 sample packet (42.667 ms) is reported as 42 ms: 62 ms vanish. Last pts + duration - first pts gives 4.000 s. The packet span matches the decoder within 1 ms in 10 of 12 files; aac in mp4 is 8 to 11 ms longer than the decoder (its edit list skips the first 1024 samples).

ts is an estimate: for 5 s of audio ffprobe reports a stream duration of 4.7787 s and a format duration of 4.7787 s; the packets and the decoder say 5.0347 s (241664 samples). ffprobe ends the audio at the last PES time stamp (554160 ticks = 6.1573 s) plus one AAC frame, and that PES holds 13 frames: 0.256 s are missing. The video stream (one frame per PES) is exact, 4.0 s.

The 14 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1.
