| 80 | I convert a GIF to MP4: what happens to the per-frame delays, and what do `-r`, `-vf fps` and `-fps_mode` change? | Synthetic 48 x 48 two-colour GIFs of 20 frames written byte by byte in Node (own LZW encoder); every frame carries its number as six blocks, so a decoded picture names its frame. Delays of 2, 3, 4, 6 and 10 hundredths (constant), one mix cycling 2, 3, 4, 6, 10 (sum 100), one with 0, 1, 2, 1. Control: from the gif itself, frame i is on screen for exactly delay i. Constant delay d, default conversion (`ffmpeg -i in.gif -pix_fmt yuv420p out.mp4`): the mp4 is 100 / d frames per second (50, 33.333, 25, 16.667, 10), all 20 frames, duration 20 d hundredths, each frame on screen for its delay; delay 3 is 33.333 fps, not 30. The mix: ffprobe reads the gif as r_frame_rate 100, average 20; the default mp4 is 100 fps with 100 packets (each source frame repeated for its delay), 1.00 s; `-fps_mode passthrough`, `vfr` and the old `-vsync passthrough` give 20 packets with exact timestamps but a duration of 0.91 s (the last delay of 10 is stored as 1; on-screen time of the last frame 10 ms, not 100). `-r 30` on the 3 hundredth gif keeps all 20 frames, each 33.3 ms, so the clip is 0.667 s (11 percent longer); `-vf fps=30` keeps 0.6 s and drops frames 5 and 15 (18 packets). On the mix `-r 30` gives 33.3 ms to the 2, 3 and 4 hundredth frames and 66.7 ms to the 6; `fps=30` also drops frames 5 and 15. `-r 25` on the 10 hundredth gif: 50 frames, each held 80 or 120 ms in turn. A delay of 0 is read as 10 hundredths (100 ms), a delay of 1 stays 10 ms. Readers: the delay bytes of every graphic control block in Node, ffprobe (rates, packet timestamps, duration), ffmpeg decoding every frame to gray pixels with Node reading the frame number | Measured on ffmpeg 6.1.1; 8.1.2 pending (the default frame rate mode and the passthrough duration are the build specific part; the tests assert relations on other builds).
