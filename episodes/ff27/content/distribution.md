### Description

You mix a 5.1 movie down to stereo with -ac 2 and the speech is quiet, or the bass has vanished. Where did they go? We build one synthetic 5.1 file where every channel is its own sine tone, and measure what comes out of the left and the right.

Source (lavfi aevalsrc, 48 kHz, 16-bit, 3 s, amplitude 0.2 on every channel): FL 150 Hz, FR 400 Hz, FC 1000 Hz (the dialogue channel), LFE 50 Hz, BL 2500 Hz, BR 6000 Hz.

Readers: (1) ffmpeg picks one output channel, two narrow bandpass filters (Q 10) isolate one tone, astats reads the RMS, compared with the same chain on that tone in the source. (2) A Goertzel sum over the raw 16-bit samples in Node, compared with the same measure on the source. They agree within 0.05 dB on every tone that is present.

Controls: a stereo source through -ac 2 is byte for byte identical. pan=stereo|FL=0.5*FL|FR=0.5*FR reads -6.02 dB. pan=stereo|FL=LFE|FR=LFE puts the 50 Hz tone at 0.00 dB, so both readers can see a low tone.

Plain -ac 2: FL and FR land on their own side at -7.66 dB. FC lands in both outputs at -10.67 dB (3.01 dB under the fronts). BL and BR land on their own side at -10.67 dB. The LFE tone is absent from both outputs. The matrix is scaled by 1 / 2.414 (the sum of the front-left row: 1 + 0.707 + 0.707), and 20 log10(1 / 2.414) is -7.66 dB.

Why: with 0.8 in FL, FC and BL, -ac 2 peaks at -1.94 dB with 0 clipped samples. The same weights as a pan, without the scaling, peak at 0 dB with 62,880 clipped samples; in 32-bit float that pan peaks at +5.72 dB.

pan=stereo|FL=FL+0.707*FC+0.707*BL|FR=FR+0.707*FC+0.707*BR gives FL and FR 0.00 dB and FC, BL, BR -3.01 dB (7.66 dB louder than -ac 2), LFE still dropped. aresample=out_chlayout=stereo:rematrix_maxval=3 gives the same levels. aresample=out_chlayout=stereo:lfe_mix_level=1 brings the LFE back at -12.90 dB and pushes FL and FR to -9.89 dB. A custom pan with 0.5 / 0.7 / 0.3 lands on -6.02 / -3.10 / -10.46 dB.

The 17 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.
