### Description

Two AAC files, each 2 seconds long, joined with -c copy. Is the result exactly 4 seconds of sound? We count samples instead of trusting a duration label.

Each source is a 2 second sine at 44.1 kHz, 128k AAC in M4A. Asked for: 88,200 samples per file. Each file has 88 packets, a skip of 1,024 samples on the first packet, and decodes to 89,088 samples (2.0201 s). The container says 2 seconds.

The join (ffmpeg -f concat -i list.txt -c copy join.m4a): 176 packets, no skip marker, container 4.02322 s (4 s plus 1,024 samples), decoded 180,224 samples (4.0867 s).

Compared with two separate decodes (178,176 samples) the join decodes 2,048 more. Compared with the audio asked for (176,400) it decodes 3,824 more, which equals 2 x (1,024 priming + 888 padding), about 87 ms at 44.1 kHz. The numbers fit the account that the first file's padding sits inside the joined stream and the skip marker is gone.

Identical numbers on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2. No remedy is claimed.
