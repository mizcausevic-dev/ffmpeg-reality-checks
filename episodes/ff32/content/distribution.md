### Description

You want the last 3 seconds of a clip, so you write -sseof -3 before -i. What exactly comes out? We count the frames.

Source (lavfi): 10 s at 30 fps, 300 frames of 64x64 gray, each with a unique code (luma carries N mod 256, chroma carries the high part). Stored three ways: FFV1 (every frame a keyframe), lossless libx264 with -g 100 (keyframes at frames 0, 100, 200) and lossless libx264 with -g 10 (a keyframe every 10 frames).

Readers: ffprobe packets (first timestamp, last timestamp plus duration), and a decode (frame md5 matched to the source, decoded pixel codes). They agree on every output.

Re-encode, -sseof -3: exactly 90 frames, source frames 210 to 299, 3 s, on all three files. Control: -ss 7 (10 s minus 3 s) gives the same 90 frames.

With -c copy and -g 100: 100 frames, 3.333 s, starting at keyframe 200, ten more than asked. With -g 10 and -sseof -3 the keyframe at 210 is exactly 3 s from the end, so copy gives 90. With -sseof -2.9 and -g 10: the re-encode gives 87 frames (213 to 299), copy gives 90 (210 to 299). -ss 7 -c copy lands on the same 100 frames as -sseof -3 -c copy. Copying the all-keyframe FFV1 file gave 96 frames in our run, so even there the start depends on how the file is indexed.

-sseof counts back from the container duration. With a 10 s picture and a 12 s audio track, -sseof -3 keeps 3 s of audio but 1 s of picture (30 frames, 270 to 299). -sseof -30 on a 10 s file is not an error: all 300 frames come out.

The twelve tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.
