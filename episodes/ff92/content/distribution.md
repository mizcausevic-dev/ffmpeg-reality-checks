### Description

You set -g 250 on a clip with hard cuts. Where do the keyframes go, and do -sc_threshold 0, -x264-params scenecut=0 and -keyint_min 250 change it? We encode one synthetic clip five ways and read the keyframes four ways.

Bench (no real footage): a 160x120 clip of 300 frames at 25 fps, written by Node as raw yuv420p. Inside a scene the picture is a field of per pixel noise that slides one pixel per frame (cheap to predict); at frames 60, 130 and 210 a hard cut puts a different field in its place (found again from the raw bytes). A control clip has no cut. libx264 with the default preset (B frames, closed GOP) and -g 250: the defaults, -sc_threshold 0, -x264-params scenecut=0, -keyint_min 250, -x264-params keyint=250:min-keyint=250, and a sweep of -keyint_min 25, 60, 61, 126 and 250.

Readers (the same file, four ways): ffprobe packets (the K flag); ffprobe frames (key_frame and pict_type I); Node reading the raw H.264 bytes (NAL type 5 slices are IDR, and the slice_type of every slice); and x264's own summary (frame I:N). They agree in all 15 runs: the keyframe readers on the same frames, the I frame readers on the same frames.

Results (ffmpeg 6.1.1): by default every cut becomes a keyframe, 0, 60, 130 and 210, and there is no keyframe at 250 (the last one is at 210, the next is due at 460). -sc_threshold 0 and -x264-params scenecut=0 are the same file (the same size for every packet): keyframes only at 0 and 250, none at the cuts, and a keyframe in the middle of the last scene; the file is 9% bigger (85876 bytes against 78511). Without a cut every setting gives 0 and 250.

-keyint_min 250 (and -x264-params keyint=250:min-keyint=250, the same file) does not switch scenecut off. The cuts are still coded as I frames, 4 in all (0, 60, 130, 210), but only 0 and 130 are keyframes: the I frames at 60 and 210 have no K flag, no key_frame, and are NAL type 1, not 5. A count of I frames says 4, a count of K flags says 2.

The rule from the sweep: an I frame at a cut is a keyframe only if it is at least min-keyint frames after the last keyframe. 25 and 60 give 4 keyframes, 61 gives 3 (the cut at 60 is one frame short), 126 gives 2 (210 is 80 after 130), and 250 gives the same file as 126.

The 10 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1.
