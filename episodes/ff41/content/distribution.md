### Description

Is -q:v 2 better than -q:v 5 for JPEG frames in FFmpeg, and why is 2 not "quality 2"? The mjpeg scale runs the other way: lower is finer and bigger. We encode one detailed synthetic frame at every setting and measure.

Source (lavfi): one 640x360 frame, testsrc2 plus deterministic noise, stored as a lossless PNG. Every JPEG is written by ffmpeg's own native mjpeg encoder with -pix_fmt yuvj444p, single frame.

Readers: file size from the filesystem and from ffprobe; quality from the ffmpeg psnr filter and from a PSNR computed in Node from raw decoded rgb24 bytes; the quantizer table (DQT marker) parsed from the JPEG bytes; decoded pixels as a Node md5 and as ffmpeg framemd5. The two PSNR readers agree within 0.1 dB on every output.

Control: -q:v 2 and -q:v 31 differ clearly in every reader. Setting 2: 149832 bytes, 36.38 dB. Setting 5: 44436 bytes, 31.78 dB. Setting 10: 22760 bytes, 30.27 dB. Setting 31: 11681 bytes, 26.97 dB. Sizes never grow and PSNR never rises from 2 to 31.

The number is a quantizer step, not a quality: the first AC step in the DQT table is 4, 10, 20 and 62 for settings 2, 5, 10 and 31, twice the setting.

The curve is steep at the low end. 2 costs 3.37x the bytes of 5 for +4.60 dB. 5 to 10 costs 1.95x for +1.52 dB. 10 to 31 costs 1.95x for +3.29 dB.

-q:v 1 is the -q:v 2 file: same bytes, same pixels, because the default minimum is 2. With -qmin 1 -q:v 1 the file is 254864 bytes at 41.59 dB (quantizer step 2). -qmin and -qmax win over -q:v: -q:v 5 -qmin 10 is the -q:v 10 file, and -q:v 5 -qmax 3 is the -q:v 3 file. -qscale:v 5 is the -q:v 5 file, byte for byte.

-huffman default is bigger than -huffman optimal, which is the default: 49679 against 44436 bytes at setting 5 (+11.8%), with identical decoded pixels in both readers.

The sixteen tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1.
