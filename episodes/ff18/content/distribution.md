### Description

You make audio louder with FFmpeg: -af volume=6dB. Does it clip? And if you boost, then cut by the same amount, do you get your audio back? We run one synthetic tone through nine gain chains and read each file two ways.

Source: a 2 second, 440 Hz sine at -3 dBFS (48 kHz, 16-bit), made by lavfi. Nothing in it is at full scale.

Readers: ffmpeg astats (peak level and flat factor, a measure of flat tops) and the raw 16-bit samples read in JavaScript (count at full scale, and largest error against the source).

Plain gain: -6 dB and +3 dB do not clip (+3 dB lands just under full scale). +6 dB puts 49.8% of samples at full scale. +12 dB puts 76.8% there. Both readers agree.

Boost 12 dB then cut 12 dB: in ONE command the source comes back exactly (error 0), because the gain stage works in floating point and does not clip. In TWO commands with a 16-bit file between, the first file clips and the cut cannot undo it (max error about 15,000). With a 32-bit float file between, the source is exact again.

alimiter=limit=0.89:level=disabled holds the peak at -1 dB with nothing at full scale. With the default level setting, this signal still puts 801 samples at full scale.
