### Description

cropdetect says crop 1920:1072 but your picture is 1078 rows high. Where did six rows of picture go, and what does the round option actually do? We draw frames with known black bars, run the filter at nine values of round, and read the result two ways.

Fixture (no footage, no network): 1920x1080 yuv420p frames from lavfi, a gray picture (luma 126) inside black bars (luma 16), six frames at 5 fps. Ten bar layouts (top | bottom, left | right): 1 | 1, 2 | 2, 5 | 5, 3 | 10, 138 | 138, 141 | 141, 140 | 140 (an 800 row picture), no bars at all, 141 | 141 at the sides, and 38 | 38 with 141 | 141. Nine values of round: not given, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 16, 32. That is 90 runs.

Readers: (1) the crop=w:h:x:y line the filter prints, cross-checked with its frame metadata (equal in all 90 runs); (2) Node on decoded pixels: the picture found by scanning the luma plane above the filter's limit of 24 (it matches the drawn picture in all 10 layouts), then the reported crop applied with the crop filter and the output frame counted pixel by pixel (picture pixels thrown away, black pixels kept). Control: bars of 140 and 140 give an exact crop (1920:800:0:140, 0 rows lost) at the default.

Findings on ffmpeg 6.1.1: (1) A 1078 row picture at the default comes back as crop=1920:1072:0:4: 6 rows of picture lost (3 above, 3 below, 11520 pixels). (2) The default round is 16 and width and height are rounded down to a multiple of round, never to the nearest: bars of 5 and 5 lose 14 rows (1070 becomes 1056), and a frame with no bars at all loses 8 rows (1080 becomes 1072). The default loses picture in 9 of the 10 layouts (rows plus columns lost: 6, 4, 14, 11, 4, 14, 0, 8, 14, 18). (3) In all 90 runs the crop never keeps a black pixel: it errs only inside the picture. (4) round=0 and round=1 are not exact: in all 10 layouts they give exactly the same crop as the default 16. round=3 gives multiples of 6, round=32 on the 1078 row picture gives 1920:1056:0:12 (22 rows lost). (5) round=2 is the exact setting, but x and y are always even: each odd bar costs one row or column of picture (1 | 1 loses 2 rows, 3 | 10 loses 1 row, 141 | 141 at the sides loses 2 columns, even bars lose nothing). gray, yuv444p and yuv420p frames give the same crop, so it is not the pixel format.

The 13 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1.
