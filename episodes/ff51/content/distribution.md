# FF51 distribution kit: FFmpeg -c copy of mov_text to MKV Writes 0 of 3 Streams; -c:s srt Works

All numbers below come from the synthetic fixture in `episodes/ff51/fixture/` (experiment.mjs, run.mjs) and its tests, measured on ffmpeg 6.1.1; on 8.1.2 only the checks that do not depend on the build were run.

### Description

You copy an mp4 into an mkv with -c copy and its subtitles are mov_text. Does it fail, or does it change? We build a five second synthetic clip with three subtitle cues, copy it nine ways on ffmpeg 6.1.1, and read what is left in the stream list.

Clip (lavfi, x264 + pcm_s16le + subtitle, 96x72, 25 fps, 5 s): three cues written by Node as an srt file, "cue one alpha" 0.500 to 1.734 s, "cue two bravo" in italics 2.000 to 3.456 s, "cue three charlie" 3.500 to 4.000 s. One source stores them as mov_text in an mp4, the other as subrip in an mkv.

Readers: (1) ffprobe, the stream list and the subtitle packet times; (2) the raw bytes read in Node, the container codec id (tx3g in an mp4 or mov, S_TEXT/UTF8 or S_TEXT/ASS in an mkv) and the cue text itself; (3) ffmpeg decoding the subtitle back to srt, parsed in Node. They agree on every output.

Controls: mov_text copied into .mov stays mov_text, and subrip remuxed into .mkv stays subrip, each with 3 streams, 3 cues, the same times and the italics. Known positive for absence: -map 0:v -map 0:a -c copy writes 2 streams and both readers find no cue text.

-c copy from the mp4 into .mkv: ffmpeg stops (the matroska muxer does not accept mov_text) and leaves a 293 byte stub with no readable stream. 0 of 3 streams, no cue text, although only the subtitle is the problem. -c:s srt and -c:s ass both work: 3 streams, video and audio copied, 3 cues, the source times to the millisecond, the italics kept (<i> in srt, an ASS override in ass). With no codec options at all, nothing fails and everything is converted: the subtitle becomes ass, the audio becomes vorbis, and the cues land 3 ms late (0.503 s, not 0.500 s).

The other direction: subrip from the mkv into .mp4 with -c copy fails and writes 0 bytes; -c copy -c:s mov_text works, with 3 cues and the same times. mov_text stores 6 packets for 3 cues (3 are empty gap packets), subrip and ass in matroska store 3.

Measured on ffmpeg 6.1.1. The 13 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1. They also pass on 8.1.2, but there the build-dependent claims (exact counts, bytes, error text) are not asserted, so the film's numbers are 6.1.1 numbers.
