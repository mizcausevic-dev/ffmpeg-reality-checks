# FF89 distribution kit: FFmpeg Transparent Video: PNG, FFV1, ProRes 4444 Keep Alpha, H.264 Drops It, VP9 Needs libvpx

All numbers below come from the synthetic fixture in `episodes/ff89/fixture/` (experiment.mjs, run.mjs) and its tests, measured on ffmpeg 6.1.1; the tests also pass on 8.1.2 with one difference: libvpx-vp9 refuses rgba input there (it picks gbrap, "not widely supported") until -pix_fmt yuva420p is named, where 6.1.1 converts by itself.

### Description

You export a clip with a see through background. Which formats keep the transparency, and does ffmpeg give it back when you read the file? We encode one synthetic card 13 ways and read the alpha two ways, plus the bytes of the file.

Bench (no real footage): one 64x48 rgba card of 8 columns of alpha (0, 36, 73, 109, 146, 182, 219, 255) and 4 rows of color (orange, indigo, lime, white), 8 identical frames, written by Node as a raw file. The color under alpha 0 is the card color on purpose. Encoders: png and ffv1 in mkv, qtrle in mov, prores_ks 4444 and with no profile and HQ in mov, libvpx-vp9 lossless and lossy and with -pix_fmt yuv420p in webm, libvpx-vp9 in mp4, libvpx (vp8) in webm, libx264 in mp4 (and with -pix_fmt yuva420p asked for).

Readers of the picture: ffmpeg to raw rgba, ffmpeg to PNG files decoded by a decoder written in Node (they agree on all 18 decodes), and alphaextract to raw gray (the alpha plane in its own pixel format; it matches the rgba alpha, ProRes within 1). Readers of the file: ffprobe (pix_fmt, alpha_mode tag) and Node reading the bytes: Matroska BlockAdditional elements in webm and mkv, the alpha_info field in every ProRes frame header, the color type of the PNG pictures in the mkv. Control: PNG, FFV1 and QuickTime Animation return all 8 alpha levels and every color exactly.

Kept (ffmpeg 6.1.1): PNG, FFV1 (bgra) and qtrle (argb) exactly. ProRes 4444 returns 8 levels within 1 (alpha_info 2 and 4:4:4 in 8 of 8 frame headers); with no -profile:v at all, prores_ks picks the same file. The off by one (ProRes: 109 to 110, 146 to 147, 182 to 183, 219 to 220) is mostly the pixel format conversion: rgba to yuva420p, yuva444p12le or gbrap and back, with no codec at all, already moves 146, 182 and 219 up by one.

Lost: libx264 never keeps alpha, and -pix_fmt yuva420p is answered with yuv420p at exit status 0. ProRes HQ (-profile:v 3) warns that the profile will not encode alpha, writes alpha_info 0 in every header, and decodes opaque. VP9 in mp4 has no alpha_mode and decodes opaque even with the libvpx-vp9 decoder. VP9 with -pix_fmt yuv420p has 0 alpha blocks in the file. In every opaque decode the transparent orange column comes back as the color that was under it (229,139,90 in H.264, within 2 levels in the others), where a player should show the gray 128,128,128.

The odd one: VP9 and VP8 in webm store the alpha as one BlockAdditional per frame (8 of 8, 179 bytes for VP9), and ffprobe reports pix_fmt yuv420p with alpha_mode 1 in the tags. ffmpeg's own VP9 decoder returns 1 alpha level (255 only); the libvpx-vp9 decoder returns 8 within 1. libvpx (VP8) refuses to encode alpha with its default auto-alt-ref (an error and no file) and works with -auto-alt-ref 0. 4:2:0 color: VP9 brings colors back up to 14 levels off at the edges of the color rows, the others within 1 (H.264 4:4:4) or exact.

To bake transparency into an opaque format on purpose: overlay on a color, then encode. The H.264 result is opaque and within 1 of the exact composite in Node.

The 14 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1.
