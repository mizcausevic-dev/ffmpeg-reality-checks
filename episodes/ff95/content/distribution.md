### Description

You pick a curve for a fade, curve=qsin, curve=exp, curve=log, and the name sounds like a shape. What gain does each of the 23 afade curves actually give a quarter, halfway and three quarters of the way through the fade? We fade a constant and read every sample.

Input (Node-made, piped to ffmpeg as f32le, 48000 Hz): a constant 0.5, so every output sample is the gain times 0.5. Command: ffmpeg -f f32le -ar 48000 -ac 1 -i in.raw -af afade=t=in:ss=0:ns=48000:curve=NAME -f f32le out.raw. The gain at 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 percent is read at samples 0, 12000, 24000, 36000 and 47999.

Readers: the raw samples in Node (gain per sample), and astats (reset=1, one frame per 4800 samples, Overall.RMS_level through ametadata) on a 1000 Hz sine, amplitude 0.5, put through the same fade. In all 23 curves and 10 blocks each (230 blocks) astats equals the Node RMS of the decoded samples within 0.001 dB (the largest difference is 0.0000005 dB). The unfaded sine reads -9.0309 dB in both. Known positive: volume=0.25 reads as gain 0.25 on every sample and -21.07 dB in astats.

Gain at 25, 50 and 75 percent: tri 0.2500, 0.5000, 0.7500. qsin 0.3827, 0.7071, 0.9239. hsin 0.1464, 0.5000, 0.8536. log 0.8796, 0.9398, 0.9750. qua 0.0625, 0.2500, 0.5625. cub 0.0156, 0.1250, 0.4219. squ 0.5000, 0.7071, 0.8660. exp 0.0002, 0.0032, 0.0562. quat 0.0039, 0.0625, 0.3164. quatr 0.7071, 0.8409, 0.9306. par 0.1340, 0.2929, 0.5000. ipar 0.4375, 0.7500, 0.9375. esin 0.2270, 0.2929, 0.3656. The other ten are in the evidence file.

Halfway the gain runs from 0.0032 (exp, -50.0 dB) to 0.9398 (log, -0.54 dB): 49.5 dB apart. Seven curves give exactly 0.5: tri, hsin, qsin2, ihsin, dese, desi and losi.

The names against the shapes: 20 curves follow a closed form to 0.0001 over all 48000 samples (qua is a squared; par is 1 minus the square root of 1 minus a; ipar is 1 minus (1 minus a) squared; exp is 10 to the power 5 times (a minus 1); log is 1 plus 0.2 times log10 of a). Seven pairs are inverse functions of each other over the whole fade: qsin and iqsin, hsin and ihsin, qua and squ, cub and cbr, quat and quatr, par and ipar, exp and log; sinc and isinc are not. qsin2 and hsin are the same curve (largest difference 1e-16).

Odd corners: esin has a shelf, 0.2885 at 40 percent and 0.2974 at 60 percent. The last sample of the fade is below 1 for par (0.9954), iqsin (0.9959), ihsin (0.9971) and dese (0.9827); the next sample is exactly 1. Some curves start (and end a fade out) with a step: log 0.0638 (-23.9 dB), quatr 0.0676 (-23.4 dB), dese 0.0173, cbr 0.0275, squ 0.0046, ihsin 0.0029, then silence. exp starts at 0.00001 (-100 dB) and a fade out with exp leaves 0.00001 after the fade instead of 0.

A fade out is the fade in played backwards, within one sample. st=0.5:d=1 gives exactly the same samples as ss=24000:ns=48000 at 48000 Hz.

The 16 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1.

The exact gains are the parts most likely to differ between builds (the tests only assert relations and a 0.0001 fit to the formulas on other builds). Not tested: builds other than 6.1.1 and 8.1.2, other fade lengths or sample rates, stereo, s16 input, real music, the dbfs-based filters.
