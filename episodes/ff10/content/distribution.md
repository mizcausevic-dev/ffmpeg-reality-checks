### Description

You have a video with two audio tracks and you convert it with a plain FFmpeg command. The output has one. Where did the other go? We build a synthetic test video and measure what comes out.

The source: one video stream and two audio streams, a 440 Hz tone (track 1, mono, default) and an 880 Hz tone (track 2). We read the results two ways: ffprobe lists the streams, and we decode each audio track and measure its pitch by counting zero crossings.

No -map option: the output has exactly 1 audio track, the 440 Hz one. The 880 Hz track is gone and FFmpeg printed no error. -c copy gives the same result.

Say what you want: -map 0 keeps both audio tracks (440 Hz and 880 Hz present). -map 0:v -map 0:a:1 keeps only the second one (880 Hz).

The documentation's automatic stream selection section says that for audio, the stream with the most channels is picked. We made track 2 stereo and track 1 mono; the output still kept track 1, which carried the default flag. When the default flag is moved to the stereo track, the output keeps that track instead (2 channels, 880 Hz). On both builds tested, the default flag won over channel count. The docs sentence does not mention it.

Same results on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.
