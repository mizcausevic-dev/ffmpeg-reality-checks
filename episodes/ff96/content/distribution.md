# FF96 distribution kit: FFmpeg loudnorm Hits the Loudness Target but Leaves Quiet Parts Over 20 dB Down

### Description

You want one volume for a whole file: a quiet interview and a loud intro, and listeners keep reaching for the knob. ffmpeg has two filters for it, loudnorm and dynaudnorm. Which one hits a loudness target, and which one really evens out quiet and loud parts? We measured both.

Input (Node-made, piped to ffmpeg as f32le mono, 48000 Hz): a speech like program of eight sections of a 140 Hz tone with 8 harmonics and a syllable envelope, at -38, -20, -32, -14, -40, -18, -26 and -12 dB RMS, with 0.4 s of silence between. Two programs: sections of 4 s (35.2 s) and of 16 s (131.2 s). The input reads -17.0 LUFS with a loudness range of 19.5 LU, and the quietest and loudest sections are 28.0 LU apart.

Readers: ebur128 (integrated loudness, loudness range, sample peak, true peak), and a BS.1770 meter written in Node (K weighting, 400 ms blocks with the -70 and -10 LU gates, 3 s blocks every 100 ms for the range, loudness of each section). They agree within 0.15 LU on integrated loudness in all 14 outputs and within 0.3 LU on the range in 13 of 14. A 1000 Hz sine reads -9.02 in Node and -9.0 in ebur128, and 6.02 dB lower reads 6.02 lower in both.

Target: loudnorm with its defaults (-24 LUFS) lands at -23.1 on both programs. loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-1.5:LRA=11 lands at -16.0 and -15.8 with the true peak on its -1.5 limit. dynaudnorm has no target: the same defaults end at -15.9 on the 4 s program and -14.0 on the 16 s one.

Gap: after loudnorm the quietest section is still 24.9 LU (4 s program) and 23.0 LU (16 s program) below the loudest, and 25.1 and 24.2 with the target of -16. The loudness range reads 9.7 on the short program (the 3 s windows blur 4 s sections) and 16.9 to 22.5 on the long one, above the range target of 7. dynaudnorm with its defaults leaves 26.3 and 20.2 LU and a range of 14.6 and 17.9; with f=150:g=5 the sections are 8.4 and 8.5 LU apart and the range is 6.7 and 8.4.

Rate: loudnorm in dynamic mode writes 192000 Hz (wav header and ffprobe agree), 4 times the samples; aresample=48000 after it brings it back. Dynaudnorm and loudnorm in linear mode keep 48000 Hz.

Two pass: with measured_I, measured_LRA, measured_TP, measured_thresh, offset and linear=true, and a range target of 20 above the input range, loudnorm stays linear: the range and the section gap are the input's (19.5 and 28.0) and the integrated loudness lands at -24.5 (4 s program) and -24.1 (16 s), off by exactly what its own pass 1 reading got wrong (-16.46 against -17.0 and -16.78 against -16.9). With a range target of 11 the second pass report says dynamic and the output is not the input shifted (-15.7 and -16.1).

The range number is knife edge: on the long program the default loudnorm output has a cluster of blocks beside the relative gate, and ebur128 reads 22.5 where Node reads 16.9.

The 15 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1.
