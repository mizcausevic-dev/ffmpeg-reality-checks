### Description

You mix two tracks with amix. The mix is quieter than either track alone, and when the shorter track ends the level changes. We mix synthetic sine tones of known level and measure each tone in the output over time.

Sources (lavfi aevalsrc, 48 kHz mono, 32-bit float): tone A 440 Hz for 6 s, tone B 660 Hz for 3 s, tone C 880 Hz for 6 s, all at -6.00 dBFS. A hot pair, 440 Hz and 660 Hz at -3 dBFS for 4 s, tests clipping.

Readers, in 0.5 s windows: a narrow bandpass around each tone with ffmpeg astats RMS, and a Goertzel on the raw float samples in Node. They agree within 0.15 dB in every window where a tone is present.

Default amix, two inputs: each tone is -12.02 dBFS, 6.02 dB below its source. Three inputs: -15.54 dBFS each (9.54 dB down), which matches scaling each input by 1/N. Controls: one input through amix=inputs=1 is unchanged (-6.00 dB, error 0), and the same tone twice comes back as exactly one input (error 0).

The jump: when tone B ends at 3.0 s, tone A rises from -12.02 to -6.00 dBFS, plus 6.02 dB, over the dropout_transition. Default 2 s: back at -6.00 at 5.0 s. dropout_transition=0: at 3.0 s. =1: at 4.0 s. =5: -9.21 dBFS at the end of the 6 s file.

normalize=0: nothing is scaled. Each tone stays at -6.00 dBFS, tone A does not move when B ends (no jump), and the same tone twice is double (+6.02 dB). It sums, so it can clip: two -3 dBFS tones peak at +2.60 dBFS in float, and in 16-bit 18.4% of the samples (35,360 of 192,000) sit at full scale. Default amix on the same pair peaks at -3.42 dBFS with no clipped samples.

weights='2 1' with the default normalize: -9.52 and -15.54 dBFS, and tone A is back at -6.00 at 4.3 s. weights='1 0.5' with normalize=0: -6.00 and -12.02 dBFS. duration=shortest, or first when the short input is first, ends at 3 s; longest (default) and first with the long input first end at 6 s.

The twelve tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.
