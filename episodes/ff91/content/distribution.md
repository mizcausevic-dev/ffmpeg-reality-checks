# FF91 distribution kit: FFmpeg -f segment -c copy: -segment_time 2 Gives 2.1, 2.5, 3.3 and 2.1 s on Uneven Keyframes

All numbers below come from the synthetic fixture in `episodes/ff91/fixture/` (experiment.mjs, run.mjs) and its tests, measured on ffmpeg 6.1.1; also run on 8.1.2 with the same results (the fixture output matches the 6.1.1 output; the tests pass in 3 of 3 runs).

### Description

You split a video with -f segment -c copy -segment_time 2. How long are the segments, and which frame does each start on? We cut one synthetic clip with uneven keyframes 27 ways and read every segment three ways.

Bench (no real footage): a 64x48 clip of 100 frames at 10 fps, no B frames, lossless libx264, with keyframes forced at frames 0, 17, 21, 46, 58 and 79 (0, 1.7, 2.1, 4.6, 5.8 and 7.9 s). Node writes the raw frames so that frame n has luma 16 + 2n: a decoded picture names itself. Control clip: a keyframe every 1.0 s. Containers: mkv, mp4 and ts. Cuts: -segment_time 2, 3 and 5, and -segment_times 2,3,5, each with and without -reset_timestamps 1.

Readers: ffprobe on every segment (start_time, duration, packet pts_time and key flags); the decoded pictures of every segment read in Node (first frame, count), joined in order by Node; and the segment list the muxer writes (csv: name, start, end). Joined files: the ts segments as bytes, the mkv and mp4 segments with the concat demuxer, read again with ffprobe.

Results (ffmpeg 6.1.1): a segment always starts on a keyframe, and a cut waits for the first keyframe at or after the next multiple of the request. -segment_time 2 gives 4 segments of 2.1, 2.5, 3.3 and 2.1 s (five were expected); 3 gives 4.6, 3.3 and 2.1 s; 5 gives 5.8 and 4.2 s. With a keyframe every second the same command gives 2.0 s five times, and 3 gives 3.0, 3.0, 3.0 and 1.0 s.

-segment_times 2,3,5 cuts at the first keyframe at or after each time: 2 gives 2.1, 3 gives 4.6 (there is no keyframe between 2.1 and 4.6), 5 gives 5.8. Four segments of 2.1, 2.5, 1.2 and 4.2 s.

-reset_timestamps 1 restarts the clock of every segment at 0 in mkv and mp4, and at 1.4 s in ts (the mpegts muxer start). Without it a segment keeps its place on the source clock. The segment list stays on the source clock either way, and its second column is the end time of the segment, not its length.

Nothing is lost: in all 27 runs the decoded frames of the segments, joined in Node, are frames 0 to 99 in order. Joined again as files: reset mkv and mp4 segments give one clock from 0 to 9.9 s; ts segments joined as bytes jump back to 1.4 s at every boundary when they were reset (3 jumps for 4 segments). mkv segments without a reset, through the concat demuxer, stretch the clock to 16.6 s with a gap of 4.7 s, because the duration of such a segment is its end time (2.1, 4.6, 7.9, 10.0); mp4 and ts report the length.

The 11 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1.
