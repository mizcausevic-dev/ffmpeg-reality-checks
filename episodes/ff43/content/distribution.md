### Description

Does libopus really resample everything to 48 kHz? ffprobe says 48000 on every Opus file. Did the encoder run at 48000? We encode a 1000 Hz sine at ten input rates and measure the stream sample rate, the sample count, the pitch and the bandwidth.

Files (lavfi sine, 2 s, mono, -c:a libopus -b:a 64k): input rates 8000, 11025, 12000, 16000, 22050, 24000, 32000, 44100, 48000 and 96000 Hz, each written to Ogg Opus, Matroska (mka) and WebM: 30 files. Control: the same sines written with pcm_s16le and flac into mka (20 files), which keep the rate.

Readers: ffprobe (stream sample_rate, format duration), and Node on the files themselves: the decoded float samples (count, pitch by rising zero crossings and by an FFT peak, band levels by a Welch FFT), the input-rate field in the OpusHead header bytes, and the last Ogg page granule position.

At the label level the answer is yes. ffprobe reports sample_rate 48000 for 30 of 30 files. Every file decodes to 96000 samples (2 s at 48000), the Ogg granule position ends at 96312 (96000 plus a pre-skip of 312), and the pitch is 1000 Hz by zero crossings and by FFT. The controls keep their own rate in 20 of 20 files (for example 8000 Hz gives 16000 samples, 96000 Hz gives 192000).

But the OpusHead input-rate field, which ffprobe does not show, holds the rate the encoder used, and it is the same in ogg, mka and webm. 8000, 12000, 16000, 24000 and 48000 are recorded as they are. 11025 is recorded as 12000, 22050 as 24000, 32000 as 24000, 44100 and 96000 as 48000: ffmpeg picks the nearest of the five rates libopus accepts.

That costs bandwidth. White noise at 32000 Hz, encoded at 128k: the 13 to 15.5 kHz band is 80 dB below the 0.3 to 9 kHz band (22.9 dB against -57.1 dB), while ffprobe still says 48000. With -ar 48000 the loss is 0.7 dB and the header says 48000. A 44100 Hz source loses 0 dB. -ar 24000 reproduces the default for 32000 Hz (80 dB) and throws the band away from a 44100 Hz source (79.2 dB).

-ar on a 48000 Hz noise source keeps the stream at 48000 and 96000 samples but band-limits it: with -ar 8000, 12000, 16000 and 24000 the level above 1.25 times the encoder band edge is 86.6, 92.5, 97.1 and 100.4 dB under the level below 0.75 times it (the 5 to 20 kHz band is -65.5 dB at -ar 8000 against 21.1 dB with no -ar). -ar 22050, 32000, 44100 and 96000 are refused: non-zero exit, no output file, although the same 32000, 44100 and 96000 inputs encode without -ar.

The sixteen tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.
