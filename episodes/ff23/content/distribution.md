### Description

You want five seconds of a video, from second 5 to second 10, so you write -ss 5 and -to 10. How long is the clip? It depends on where the options go. We cut one synthetic source five ways and check exactly which frames came out.

Source: 20 seconds, 500 frames, 25 fps, 320x180, made by lavfi testsrc2 and stored losslessly (FFV1). All 500 frames are different, so a frame md5 identifies a frame. Every cut is written losslessly too.

Readers: ffprobe (frames decoded, and duration) and frame md5 matched back to the source (which seconds of the source the clip holds).

-ss 5 -to 10 -i (both before the input): 5 s, 125 frames, source 5 to 10. -i -ss 5 -to 10 (both after): the same.

-ss 5 -i in.mkv -to 10 (-to after the input): 10 s, 250 frames, source 5 to 15. After a seek the output clock restarts at 0, so -to 10 means 10 s on that new clock.

-ss 5 -i in.mkv -t 5: 5 s, source 5 to 10 (-t is a length). -to 10 -i alone: the first 10 s of the source.

Both readers agree on all five cuts. The seven tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2.
