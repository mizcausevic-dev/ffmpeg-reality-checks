### Description

A phone records a video sideways and adds a rotation tag that says: turn this 90 degrees. Does FFmpeg follow the tag when you convert the file? We build a synthetic test clip with that tag, run five commands, and read each result two ways.

The source: a 320x180 test pattern, stored sideways, with a display rotation tag of 90. Readers: ffprobe (stored size and tag), and a decode of the video compared against four reference pictures (as recorded, upright, upside down, turned the other way) with the PSNR filter.

A plain re-encode turns the pixels for you: stored 180x320, no tag left, decoded picture upright. -c copy keeps the sideways pixels and the tag, and the decoded picture is still upright.

The trap: adding -vf transpose=1 to fix a sideways video. FFmpeg already turned it, so your filter turns it back. Stored 320x180, no tag, and the decoded picture matches the sideways original.

-noautorotate depends on the build. On ffmpeg 6.1.1 the tag is carried to the output and the picture is upright. On ffmpeg 8.1.2 the tag is dropped and the video plays sideways. Turning it by hand with -noautorotate -vf transpose=2: 6.1.1 ends upside down (tag kept, turned twice), 8.1.2 ends upright.

Let FFmpeg turn it, or copy the streams. Do not add transpose on top of a tagged video.
