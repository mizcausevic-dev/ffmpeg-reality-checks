### Description

You scale to 1920x1080 with scale=1920:1080:force_original_aspect_ratio=decrease, then pad=1920:1080:(ow-iw)/2:(oh-ih)/2, and a black line appears, or the encoder complains about an odd size. Why? We measure where the picture starts and ends.

Source (lavfi testsrc2, one frame, lossless, yuv420p): 1280x690. Control: a 4:3 source (640x480) scales to 1440x1080 and the pad gives 240 black columns on each side and none above or below.

Readers: ffprobe width and height (of the padded output and of the same graph without pad), and a decode read in Node (the rows and columns that are all black, and a row by row comparison with the unpadded render). They agree on every case.

Odd height: scale makes 1920x1035, so the pad expression asks for y=22.5. The output is 1920x1080, but only 1034 picture rows are in it: the bars are 22 above and 24 below, and the missing row is the last one.

Why: in 4:2:0 pad rounds the offset down to an even number and crops the picture to an even width and height. pad=1920:1080:0:21 puts the picture at row 20. In yuv444p the same filters keep all 1035 rows and honor row 21 (bars 21 and 24). Odd width: 1278x720 scales to 1917x1080, the pad gives 0 columns on the left and 4 on the right, with 1916 picture columns. Even size, odd offset: a 4:3 source in a 1080x1920 frame is 1080x810, asks for y=555, nothing is cropped, bars are 554 above and 556 below.

libx264 with yuv420p: scale alone to 1920x1035 fails with "height not divisible by 2 (1920x1035)"; 1917x1080 fails with "width not divisible by 2". The padded 1920x1080 encodes. Adding force_divisible_by=2 to scale gives 1920x1036, nothing cropped, bars 22 and 22, and the encoder accepts it. The scale filter then writes a sample aspect ratio of 1036:1035. An odd offset is still not centered: 1278x720 with force_divisible_by=2 is 1918 wide, x=1, so the bars are 0 and 2 columns.

The nineteen tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1.
