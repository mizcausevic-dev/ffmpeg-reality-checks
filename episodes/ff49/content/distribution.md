### Description

You encode an MP3 with libmp3lame and -q:a 2. What bitrate do you get? Quality is a number from 0 to 9, not a bitrate. Does one setting always cost the same? We encode five synthetic signals at all ten settings, compare them with a constant bitrate control, and measure the average bitrate two independent ways.

Signals (lavfi, stereo, 44100 Hz, 10 s each): digital silence, a sine (1000 Hz left, 1500 Hz right), a chord (five tones per channel), a rising sweep, and white noise (a different seed in each channel). One more file has 5 s of silence followed by 5 s of the same noise. Each is encoded with -c:a libmp3lame at -q:a 0 to 9.

Readers: ffprobe, which gives the format bit_rate that tools print and the packet sizes (bytes over packet duration), and a Node walker that reads every mp3 frame header itself (bitrate index, padding bit, frame length) and skips the Xing or Info tag frame. They agree on all 58 files: the two averages are within 0.05 kbit/s, and the printed bit_rate is above them by less than 1 kbit/s on ffmpeg 6.1.1 (it includes the tag frame); on 8.1.2 the largest gap is 1.9 kbit/s, on the noise 320 kbit/s file.

Control: -b:a 128k gives exactly 128 kbit/s on all five signals, read by both readers, and every single frame is a 128 kbit/s frame (the file has an Info tag, not a Xing tag). -b:a 320k gives 320.

At -q:a 2 the same encoder and the same ten seconds give 32 kbit/s for silence, 43 for the sweep, 58 for the sine, 69 for the chord and 182 for white noise: 5.7 times apart from one setting. Silence is 32 kbit/s at all ten settings (every frame is the 32 kbit/s minimum, and the file size is identical). Noise falls from 250 kbit/s at quality 0 to 77 at quality 9 (3.2 times). A bigger quality number never cost more bits on any of the five signals.

Inside one file the frames differ too: at -q:a 2 the noise uses frames of 160 to 320 kbit/s, the sine 48 to 320. The 5 s silence plus 5 s noise file averages 107.4 kbit/s at quality 2, about the mean of its halves (32.7 and 182.5 read separately), and none of its 384 frames is within 25 kbit/s of that average.

The 12 tests pass on ffmpeg 6.1.1 (8.1.2 pending).
