### Description

You pick an x264 preset and keep the same CRF. Does a slower preset give better video? The FFmpeg wiki says a slower preset gives better compression, defined as quality per file size. We measure size and quality.

Two synthetic 10 second clips (a test pattern and a mandelbrot zoom), each with a lossless copy as the reference. Each is encoded at CRF 23 with eight presets, ultrafast to slower, and scored against the reference with SSIM.

Quality: across all eight presets SSIM stays between 0.9936 and 0.9972 on the test pattern, and between 0.9824 and 0.9889 on the mandelbrot clip.

Size, test pattern, ffmpeg 6.1.1: ultrafast 2,245,279 bytes, veryfast 834,521, faster 893,064, fast 915,130, medium 904,286, slow 865,578, slower 797,106. Size is not monotonic: faster, fast and medium came out bigger than veryfast. The mandelbrot clip shows the same shape.

Time: slower took 5.0 to 6.3 times as long as ultrafast, across both clips and both machines. The wiki claim that a slower preset saves bitrate at constant quality held from ultrafast to veryfast and from medium to slower on these clips, not at every step between.
