# FFmpeg Reality Checks: series outline

Format: one claim, one runnable synthetic fixture, one measured result, regression tests. Same as Web Reality Checks.

**Status key.** *Measured* means the numbers below were produced by running the command on the stated build. *Planned* means not yet run. Numbers are from **ffmpeg 6.1.1 (Ubuntu 24.04 package)** unless a row says two builds (6.1.1 and 8.1.2). Behaviour, exit codes and message text can differ on other builds, so every episode records `ffmpeg -version` in its evidence file.

| # | Claim under test | Measured result (ffmpeg 6.1.1, synthetic fixtures) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Where does `-c copy` really start? | Ask for 3 s from 7.5 s, keyframes every 5 s. `-ss` before `-i`: starts at 7.52 s, 75 frames, but the file holds 138 packets (63 hidden pre-roll). `-ss` after `-i`: starts at 10.00 s, 13 frames (0.52 s). Re-encode: starts 7.52 s, 74 frames | Measured, 5 tests pass (`episodes/ff01`) |
| 02 | Why does my MP4 not play in QuickTime or the browser? | RGB input encoded with `libx264` and no `-pix_fmt` gives `High 4:4:4 Predictive`, `yuv444p`. With `-pix_fmt yuv420p`: `High`, `yuv420p` | Measured (playback in specific players not tested) |
| 03 | Why does my video wait to download before playing? | Atom order without `+faststart`: `ftyp, free, mdat, moov`. With it: `ftyp, moov, free, mdat`. Same frames and size. ffprobe reading over loopback HTTP, client that cannot seek: whole file (plain) vs under 50 kB (faststart). Client with range requests: 3 requests (one at the index) vs 1. Same order on ffmpeg 6.1.1 and 8.1.2 | Measured, 7 tests pass (`episodes/ff03`). Browser behaviour not tested |
| 04 | Does a slower preset make better video at the same CRF? | Two synthetic 10 s clips (testsrc2, mandelbrot), CRF 23, 8 presets, SSIM against a lossless copy. Quality barely moves (0.9936 to 0.9972 on testsrc2). Size is not monotonic: veryfast is smaller than faster, fast, medium and slow on both clips. Slower takes 5.0 to 6.3 times as long as ultrafast. Same shape on x264 core 164 (ffmpeg 6.1.1) and 165 (8.1.2) | Measured, 8 tests pass (`episodes/ff04`). Real footage, hardware encoders not tested |
| 05 | What does "constant frame rate" do to a phone video? | Synthetic VFR clip: 93 frames over 5.0 s. Writing MP4 with no frame rate option: ffmpeg 6.1.1 gives 150 frames (57 duplicates), ffmpeg 8.1.2 keeps 93 and the uneven gaps. `-fps_mode passthrough`: 93 on both. `-fps_mode cfr -r 30`: 150 (6.1.1) or 151 (8.1.2). 93 distinct pictures in every variant, two counters agree, length stays about 5 s | Measured on two builds, 7 tests pass (`episodes/ff05`). The default differs by build; builds in between and real phone files not tested |
| 06 | Can I join clips with `-c copy` if they look the same? | Two 2 s clips, 320x180 and 640x360. Concat demuxer with `-c copy`: exit 0, no warning, container says 320x180, but frames inside are 50 at 320x180 and 50 at 640x360. Matching clips: 4.0 s, no errors | Measured |
| 07 | Does `loudnorm` hit the target without changing the sound? | Two-level tone (quiet and loud halves), target -14 LUFS, source -26.1 LUFS, LRA 8.0 LU. One pass (dynamic): -11.7 LUFS, LRA 4.5 LU, so it misses the target and flattens the dynamics. Two pass (`linear=true`): -14.5 LUFS, LRA 8.0 LU, so the shape is kept. | Measured on a synthetic tone, not music. Tests in `episodes/ff07` |
| 08 | Does joining AAC files with `-c copy` keep the length? | Two 2.0 s AAC files at 44.1 kHz. Each decodes to 89,088 samples (2.0201 s). The join decodes to 180,224 samples (4.0867 s) against 176,400 valid samples: 3,824 extra, which is 2 x (1,024 priming + 888 padding). Container duration of the join: 4.0232 s. | Measured, extra samples accounted for from packet data. Tests in `episodes/ff08` |
| 09 | Why does `scale=-1:720` sometimes crash? | `scale=-1:333` on 1000x562 gives 593x333, libx264 refuses ("width not divisible by 2"), exit 187 on Linux 6.1.1 (a different number on Windows 8.1.2), nothing usable written. `scale=-2:333` also fails (333 is odd). `scale=-2:334` gives 594x334 and works. The same frame encodes with `-pix_fmt yuv444p` | Measured on two builds. libx264 only. Tests in `episodes/ff09` |
| 10 | Where did my second audio track go? | Source with a 440 Hz mono default track and an 880 Hz track. No `-map`: 1 audio track (440 Hz). `-c copy` same. `-map 0`: both. `-map 0:v -map 0:a:1`: the 880 Hz track. The docs say the most channels wins, but with a stereo second track the default flag still won; moving the flag moved the winner | Measured on two builds. Subtitles, other containers not tested. Tests in `episodes/ff10` |
| 11 | Is x264 `-crf 0` really lossless? | 25 synthetic RGB frames, 320x180. FFV1 and `libx264rgb -crf 0`: 25 of 25 frames identical, PSNR inf. Plain `libx264 -crf 0` stores yuv444p: 0 of 25 identical back in RGB, PSNR 54.6 dB; 25 of 25 identical in yuv444p. With `-pix_fmt yuv420p`: PSNR vs RGB 24.0 dB (6.1.1), 22.8 dB (8.1.2) | Measured on two builds. Other lossless codecs, real footage, hardware encoders not tested. Tests in `episodes/ff11` |
| 12 | Does FFmpeg keep my video's metadata? | Synthetic MP4 with five made-up tags (title, artist, comment, location, stream handler name). Plain re-encode and `-c copy`: all five kept, read by ffprobe and by a raw byte search (location is a `loci` atom, searched by name). `-map_metadata -1`: all five gone with re-encode and `-c copy`. The format `encoder` tag (Lavf) remains until `-fflags +bitexact` | Measured on two builds. Real phone files, location tracks, thumbnails, vendor atoms, other containers not tested. Tests in `episodes/ff12` |

## Rules for every episode
1. Synthetic input only (ffmpeg `lavfi` sources). No network, no user media.
2. Prefer frame md5 (lossless source) and ffprobe over opinions.
3. Record the ffmpeg version in `evidence.json`; tests assert behaviour, not text that changes between builds, unless that text is the point.
4. State what was not tested (specific players, other builds) in the film and README.
